Ground elder

Aegopodium podagraria
Also called Skvalderkål

Ground elder is a wild green you can forage from March to June across Denmark. Garden weed with leaves split into threes on a triangular stem, forming dense patches in shade.

Caution. Its white umbels resemble poisonous hemlocks; pick leaves only, and check the triangular stem.

When is ground elder in season in Denmark?

Ground elder is usually in season March to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does ground elder grow in Denmark?

Ground elder grows across Denmark. Regions: Jutland, Funen and southern islands, Zealand, Bornholm.

How do you recognise ground elder?

Garden weed with leaves split into threes on a triangular stem, forming dense patches in shade.

How do you harvest ground elder?

Pick the shiny, folded young leaves before the plant flowers; check for the triangular, unspotted stem.

How do you eat ground elder?

Young glossy leaves like parsley or spinach; in pesto and soups.

How do you store ground elder?

Blanch and freeze.

What can be confused with ground elder?

Hemlock (Conium maculatum), rated deadly. Elder is a woody shrub; hemlock is a soft plant with hollow stems. Hemlock stems are smooth with purple blotches and smell mousy. Ground elder has leaves in threes on a triangular, unspotted stem. Hemlock water-dropwort (Oenanthe crocata), rated deadly. Grows in the same wet ditches as watercress. It has celery-like, divided leaves and white umbels; watercress has rounded leaflets and small white four-petalled flowers. Its roots look like pale fingers; one root can kill.

What is ground elder good for?

Vitamin C, Vitamin A, Iron & calcium.
