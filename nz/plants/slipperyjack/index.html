Slippery jack

Suillus luteus
Also called Slippery jack

Slippery jack is a wild mushroom you can forage from March to June across New Zealand. Brown, slimy-capped bolete under pine plantations, with yellow pores and a ring on the stem.

Caution. Intermediate mushroom: learn it in person first, peel the cap and always cook it.

When is slippery jack in season in New Zealand?

Slippery jack is usually in season March to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does slippery jack grow in New Zealand?

Slippery jack grows across New Zealand. Regions: Northland and Auckland, Central North Island, East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū, Top of the South, West Coast, Canterbury, Otago and Southland.

How do you recognise slippery jack?

Brown, slimy-capped bolete under pine plantations, with yellow pores and a ring on the stem.

How do you harvest slippery jack?

Twist or cut mushrooms at the base, brush off soil in the woods and carry them in a basket, never a plastic bag. Only take ones you're 100% sure of, leave old and very young ones, and follow local limits.

How do you eat slippery jack?

Peel the slimy skin, then fry, or dry for soups.

How do you store slippery jack?

Dry.

What can be confused with slippery jack?

Death cap (Amanita phalloides), rated deadly. Death caps grow under oaks in towns and parks. They have white gills that never turn pink or brown, a ring, and a bag-like cup at the base.

What is slippery jack good for?

Protein.
