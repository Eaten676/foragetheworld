Shaggy inkcap

Coprinus comatus
Also called Shaggy inkcap

Shaggy inkcap is a wild mushroom you can forage from March to June across New Zealand. Tall white cylinder with shaggy scales on lawns and verges; gills turn to black ink within hours.

Caution. Intermediate mushroom: learn it in person first and always cook it. The related common inkcap is poisonous with alcohol.

When is shaggy inkcap in season in New Zealand?

Shaggy inkcap is usually in season March to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does shaggy inkcap grow in New Zealand?

Shaggy inkcap grows across New Zealand. Regions: Northland and Auckland, Central North Island, East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū, Top of the South, West Coast, Canterbury, Otago and Southland.

How do you recognise shaggy inkcap?

Tall white cylinder with shaggy scales on lawns and verges; gills turn to black ink within hours.

How do you harvest shaggy inkcap?

Twist or cut mushrooms at the base, brush off soil in the woods and carry them in a basket, never a plastic bag. Only take ones you're 100% sure of, leave old and very young ones, and follow local limits.

How do you eat shaggy inkcap?

Cook the same day while gills are white: in butter or soup.

How do you store shaggy inkcap?

Doesn't keep.

What can be confused with shaggy inkcap?

Common inkcap (Coprinopsis atramentaria), rated harmful. Shaggy inkcap is tall, white and shaggy. The common inkcap is smaller, smooth and grey-brown; with alcohol it causes violent sickness.

What is shaggy inkcap good for?

Protein.
