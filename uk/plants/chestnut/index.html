Sweet chestnut

Castanea sativa
Also called Sweet chestnut

Sweet chestnut is a wild nut you can forage from October to November in South East England, South West England, Midlands and East and Wales. Large tree with long, saw-toothed leaves; glossy brown nuts inside very spiny green cases.

Caution. Horse chestnuts (conkers) are poisonous: they have one round nut in a case with few, blunt spikes.

When is sweet chestnut in season in the UK?

Sweet chestnut is usually in season October to November. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does sweet chestnut grow in the UK?

Sweet chestnut grows in East Midlands, East of England, London, South East England, South West England, Wales, West Midlands. Regions: South East England, South West England, Midlands and East, Wales.

How do you recognise sweet chestnut?

Large tree with long, saw-toothed leaves; glossy brown nuts inside very spiny green cases.

How do you harvest sweet chestnut?

Gather fallen nuts with gloves or by rolling the spiny cases open with your boots; choose plump, shiny nuts.

How do you eat sweet chestnut?

Roasted, chestnut purée, flour, stuffing.

How do you store sweet chestnut?

Freeze peeled, or dry and grind.

What can be confused with sweet chestnut?

Horse chestnut (Aesculus hippocastanum), rated poisonous. Sweet chestnut cases are covered in fine dense spines like a hedgehog and hold 2–3 flat nuts with a pointed tip. Horse chestnut cases have a few blunt spikes and hold one round conker. Horse chestnut leaves are hand-shaped; sweet chestnut leaves are single and saw-toothed.

What is sweet chestnut good for?

Calories.

What can you cook with sweet chestnut?

  • Chestnut and mushroom roast: Roasted chestnuts in a nut roast with mushrooms.
