Stinging nettle

Urtica dioica
Also called Nettle, Common nettle

Stinging nettle is a wild green you can forage from March to June across the United States. Upright plant with toothed, heart-shaped leaves covered in stinging hairs; grows in rich, damp soil near streams and old farms.

When is stinging nettle in season in the United States?

Stinging nettle is usually in season March to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does stinging nettle grow in the United States?

Stinging nettle grows across the United States. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Southwest, Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest, California, Alaska.

How do you recognise stinging nettle?

Upright plant with toothed, heart-shaped leaves covered in stinging hairs; grows in rich, damp soil near streams and old farms.

How do you harvest stinging nettle?

Wear gloves and snip the top four leaves of plants that haven't flowered.

How do you eat stinging nettle?

Soup, pesto, spanakopita filling, or tea. Pick the top four leaves with gloves.

How do you store stinging nettle?

Blanch and freeze, or dry for tea.

What is stinging nettle good for?

Protein, Iron & calcium, Vitamin A.

What can you cook with stinging nettle?

  • Nettle tea: Steep fresh or dried nettle leaves in hot water for 5–10 minutes.
  • Nettle soup: Nettle tops simmered with potato and onion, blended with cream.
  • Lamb's quarters spanakopita: Wild greens with feta in crisp filo.
