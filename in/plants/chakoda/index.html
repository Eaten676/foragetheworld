Sickle senna

Senna tora
Also called Chakoda, Takla, Thagache, Panwar

Sickle senna is a wild green you can forage from June to August in Central forests, West coast and Deccan, East, South India and Northern plains. Monsoon weed with rounded leaflets in pairs of three and small yellow flowers. A tribal staple green.

Caution. Use only the tender first leaves, always cooked, in moderate amounts. It is laxative.

When is sickle senna in season in India?

Sickle senna is usually in season June to August. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does sickle senna grow in India?

Sickle senna grows in Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Chandigarh, Chhattisgarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Delhi, Goa, Gujarat, Haryana, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Kerala, Lakshadweep, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Puducherry, Punjab, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, West Bengal. Regions: Central forests, West coast and Deccan, East, South India, Northern plains.

How do you recognise sickle senna?

Monsoon weed with rounded leaflets in pairs of three and small yellow flowers. A tribal staple green.

How do you harvest sickle senna?

Pinch off the young tips and top leaves with your fingers or scissors. Leave the lower leaves and roots so the plant regrows. Pick in the morning, away from roads and drains.

How do you eat sickle senna?

Young leaves only, as bhaji with onion and garlic.

How do you store sickle senna?

Blanch and shade-dry.

What is sickle senna good for?

Iron & calcium.

What can you cook with sickle senna?

  • Takla bhaji / chakoda bhaji: Tender senna leaves cooked dry with onion, garlic and besan.
