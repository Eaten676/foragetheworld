Kōnini

Fuchsia excorticata
Also called Kōnini, Tree fuchsia

Kōnini is a wild fruit you can forage from December to March across New Zealand. Tree fuchsia with peeling orange bark and dangling purple-red flowers; ripe berries are dark purple and sweet.

When is kōnini in season in New Zealand?

Kōnini is usually in season December to March. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does kōnini grow in New Zealand?

Kōnini grows across New Zealand. Regions: Northland and Auckland, Central North Island, East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū, Top of the South, West Coast, Canterbury, Otago and Southland.

How do you recognise kōnini?

Tree fuchsia with peeling orange bark and dangling purple-red flowers; ripe berries are dark purple and sweet.

How do you harvest kōnini?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat kōnini?

Fresh, jam.

How do you store kōnini?

Jam.

What is kōnini good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with kōnini?

  • Kōnini jam on scones: Tree fuchsia berries cooked into jam.
