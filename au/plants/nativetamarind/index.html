Native tamarind

Diploglottis australis
Also called Native tamarind

Native tamarind is a wild fruit you can forage from November to January in Subtropical east coast. Rainforest tree with huge compound leaves; hairy capsules hold orange, very tart arils.

When is native tamarind in season in Australia?

Native tamarind is usually in season November to January. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does native tamarind grow in Australia?

Native tamarind grows in New South Wales, Queensland. Regions: Subtropical east coast.

How do you recognise native tamarind?

Rainforest tree with huge compound leaves; hairy capsules hold orange, very tart arils.

How do you harvest native tamarind?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat native tamarind?

Cordial, jam, sauces.

How do you store native tamarind?

Freeze pulp.

What is native tamarind good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with native tamarind?

  • Native tamarind cordial: Tart orange arils simmered with sugar and strained.
