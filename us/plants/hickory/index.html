Shagbark hickory

Carya ovata
Also called Shagbark hickory

Shagbark hickory is a wild nut you can forage from September to October in Northeast, Southeast, Midwest and Great Plains. Tree with bark peeling in long shaggy strips; round nuts in thick four-part husks.

When is shagbark hickory in season in the United States?

Shagbark hickory is usually in season September to October. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does shagbark hickory grow in the United States?

Shagbark hickory grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains.

How do you recognise shagbark hickory?

Tree with bark peeling in long shaggy strips; round nuts in thick four-part husks.

How do you harvest shagbark hickory?

Gather nuts from the ground once they fall, discarding any with holes or mould. Leave plenty for wildlife, which depends on them for winter.

How do you eat shagbark hickory?

Like pecans; bark makes hickory syrup.

How do you store shagbark hickory?

Dry in shell.
