Autumn olive

Elaeagnus umbellata
Also called Autumn olive

Autumn olive is a wild fruit you can forage from September to November in Northeast, Southeast, Midwest and Great Plains. Invasive shrub with leaves silvery underneath and red berries speckled with silver dots.

When is autumn olive in season in the United States?

Autumn olive is usually in season September to November. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does autumn olive grow in the United States?

Autumn olive grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains.

How do you recognise autumn olive?

Invasive shrub with leaves silvery underneath and red berries speckled with silver dots.

How do you harvest autumn olive?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat autumn olive?

Fruit leather, jam, ketchup.

How do you store autumn olive?

Fruit leather.

What is autumn olive good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C.
