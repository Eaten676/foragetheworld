Karamū berries

Coprosma robusta
Also called Karamū berries

Karamū berries is a wild fruit you can forage from March to May across New Zealand. Shrub with glossy, opposite leaves and dense clusters of small orange berries.

When is karamū berries in season in New Zealand?

Karamū berries is usually in season March to May. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does karamū berries grow in New Zealand?

Karamū berries grows across New Zealand. Regions: Northland and Auckland, Central North Island, East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū, Top of the South, West Coast, Canterbury, Otago and Southland.

How do you recognise karamū berries?

Shrub with glossy, opposite leaves and dense clusters of small orange berries.

How do you harvest karamū berries?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat karamū berries?

Fresh; seeds once roasted as coffee.

How do you store karamū berries?

Best fresh.

What is karamū berries good for?

Vitamin C.
