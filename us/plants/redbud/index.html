Redbud

Cercis canadensis
Also called Eastern redbud

Redbud is a edible flower or tea you can forage from March to April in Southeast, Midwest, Northeast and Great Plains. Small tree covered in magenta pea flowers before the heart-shaped leaves appear.

When is redbud in season in the United States?

Redbud is usually in season March to April. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does redbud grow in the United States?

Redbud grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Southeast, Midwest, Northeast, Great Plains.

How do you recognise redbud?

Small tree covered in magenta pea flowers before the heart-shaped leaves appear.

How do you harvest redbud?

Pick flowers on a dry, sunny morning once the dew has gone, when the scent is strongest. Take a few heads from each plant so it can still set fruit.

How do you eat redbud?

Flowers raw on salads; young pods cooked.

How do you store redbud?

Pickle the buds.

What is redbud good for?

Vitamin C.
