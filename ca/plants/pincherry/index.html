Pin cherry

Prunus pensylvanica
Also called Pin cherry, Cerisier de Pennsylvanie

Pin cherry is a wild fruit you can forage from July to August across Canada. Slender tree of burns, clearings and roadsides with shiny reddish bark, narrow toothed leaves, and long-stemmed, bright red, pea-sized cherries.

Caution. The pit, leaves and bark are toxic: strain out pits and never crush them.

When is pin cherry in season in Canada?

Pin cherry is usually in season July to August. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does pin cherry grow in Canada?

Pin cherry grows across Canada. Regions: British Columbia, Prairies, Ontario, Quebec, Atlantic Canada, The North.

How do you recognise pin cherry?

Slender tree of burns, clearings and roadsides with shiny reddish bark, narrow toothed leaves, and long-stemmed, bright red, pea-sized cherries.

How do you harvest pin cherry?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat pin cherry?

Too sour to eat many raw: cook into jelly, syrup or juice.

How do you store pin cherry?

Jelly or syrup.

What is pin cherry good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with pin cherry?

  • Pin cherry syrup soda: Strained pin cherry syrup topped with sparkling water.
  • Pin cherry jelly: Pin cherries cooked, strained and set into a tart jelly.
