Japanese knotweed shoots

Reynoutria japonica
Also called Japansk pileurt

Japanese knotweed shoots is a root or shoot you can forage from March to May across Denmark. Invasive, bamboo-like plant with hollow, red-speckled stems and shield-shaped leaves; spring shoots look like asparagus.

Caution. Never move any part of the plant: spreading it is illegal in the UK and Ireland. Councils often spray it, so only pick where you know it's untreated.

When is Japanese knotweed shoots in season in Denmark?

Japanese knotweed shoots is usually in season March to May. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does Japanese knotweed shoots grow in Denmark?

Japanese knotweed shoots grows across Denmark. Regions: Jutland, Funen and southern islands, Zealand, Bornholm.

How do you recognise Japanese knotweed shoots?

Invasive, bamboo-like plant with hollow, red-speckled stems and shield-shaped leaves; spring shoots look like asparagus.

How do you harvest Japanese knotweed shoots?

Only dig roots where you have the landowner's permission, since uprooting wild plants is illegal in many places. Take a few from a large patch and backfill the hole.

How do you eat Japanese knotweed shoots?

Young shoots peeled and stewed like rhubarb, or in crumbles and jam.

How do you store Japanese knotweed shoots?

Stew and freeze.

What is Japanese knotweed shoots good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C.
