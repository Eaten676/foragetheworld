Daylily

Hemerocallis fulva
Also called Orange daylily, Ditch lily

Daylily is a edible flower or tea you can forage from June to July in Northeast, Southeast, Midwest and Great Plains. Clumps of long strap leaves along roadsides with big orange trumpet flowers that last a day.

Caution. Some people get sick; try a little. Never confuse with true lilies, which have leafy stems and are toxic.

When is daylily in season in the United States?

Daylily is usually in season June to July. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does daylily grow in the United States?

Daylily grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains.

How do you recognise daylily?

Clumps of long strap leaves along roadsides with big orange trumpet flowers that last a day.

How do you harvest daylily?

Pick flowers on a dry, sunny morning once the dew has gone, when the scent is strongest. Take a few heads from each plant so it can still set fruit.

How do you eat daylily?

Buds stir-fried like green beans; flowers stuffed.

How do you store daylily?

Dry buds.

What is daylily good for?

Vitamin A.
