Khejri (sangri)

Prosopis cineraria
Also called Sangri, Khejri, Shami

Khejri (sangri) is a wild green you can forage from March to May in Thar and dry northwest and West coast and Deccan. The desert's lifeline tree. Thin green pods are picked young.

When is khejri (sangri) in season in India?

Khejri (sangri) is usually in season March to May. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does khejri (sangri) grow in India?

Khejri (sangri) grows in Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Goa, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Rajasthan. Regions: Thar and dry northwest, West coast and Deccan.

How do you recognise khejri (sangri)?

The desert's lifeline tree. Thin green pods are picked young.

How do you harvest khejri (sangri)?

Pick tender young green pods before they dry and harden.

How do you eat khejri (sangri)?

Ker-sangri with ker berries.

How do you store khejri (sangri)?

Blanch and sun-dry. Dried sangri keeps a year or more.

What is khejri (sangri) good for?

Protein, Calories.

What can you cook with khejri (sangri)?

  • Ker sangri: Dried ker berries and sangri beans soaked, boiled and cooked with yoghurt, red chilli and amchur.
  • Panchkuta: The Thar's five-ingredient desert curry, cooked dry with spices and oil.
