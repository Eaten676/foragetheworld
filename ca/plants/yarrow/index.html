Yarrow

Achillea millefolium
Also called Yarrow, Achillée millefeuille

Yarrow is a edible flower or tea you can forage from June to September across Canada. Feathery, finely divided leaves and flat clusters of tiny white flowers in meadows and roadsides.

Caution. Hemlock has similar white heads but smooth, purple-blotched stems; yarrow's leaves are feather-like.

When is yarrow in season in Canada?

Yarrow is usually in season June to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does yarrow grow in Canada?

Yarrow grows across Canada. Regions: British Columbia, Prairies, Ontario, Quebec, Atlantic Canada, The North.

How do you recognise yarrow?

Feathery, finely divided leaves and flat clusters of tiny white flowers in meadows and roadsides.

How do you harvest yarrow?

Snip young leaves in spring and flower heads in summer.

How do you eat yarrow?

Young leaves in salads; tea.

How do you store yarrow?

Dry.
