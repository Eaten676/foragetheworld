Garden angelica

Angelica archangelica
Also called Kvanne

Garden angelica is a wild green you can forage from June to July across Sweden. Tall plant of northern riverbanks and coasts with hollow stems and round, green-white flower balls; strongly aromatic.

Caution. Hemlock and water hemlock are deadly look-alikes. Only pick with an experienced forager until you know it well.

When is garden angelica in season in Sweden?

Garden angelica is usually in season June to July. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does garden angelica grow in Sweden?

Garden angelica grows across Sweden. Regions: Götaland (south), Svealand (middle), Norrland (north).

How do you recognise garden angelica?

Tall plant of northern riverbanks and coasts with hollow stems and round, green-white flower balls; strongly aromatic.

How do you harvest garden angelica?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat garden angelica?

Young stems candied or cooked; leaves in fish dishes; a traditional Sámi and Norse vegetable (kvann).

How do you store garden angelica?

Candy or dry.

What can be confused with garden angelica?

Hemlock (Conium maculatum), rated deadly. Elder is a woody shrub; hemlock is a soft plant with hollow stems. Hemlock stems are smooth with purple blotches and smell mousy. Ground elder has leaves in threes on a triangular, unspotted stem.

What is garden angelica good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with garden angelica?

  • Angelica stems with fish (kvanne): Young kvanne stems cooked with char, a Sámi tradition.
