Palmyra palm

Borassus flabellifer
Also called Tadgola, Taati nungu, Nungu, Taal

Palmyra palm is a wild fruit you can forage from April to January in South India, East and West coast and Deccan. Tall fan-leaf palm on dry plains and coasts. Jelly fruit in summer, sprouted tubers in winter.

When is palmyra palm in season in India?

Palmyra palm is usually in season April to January. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does palmyra palm grow in India?

Palmyra palm grows in Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Goa, Gujarat, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Kerala, Lakshadweep, Maharashtra, Odisha, Puducherry, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, West Bengal. Regions: South India, East, West coast and Deccan.

How do you recognise palmyra palm?

Tall fan-leaf palm on dry plains and coasts. Jelly fruit in summer, sprouted tubers in winter.

How do you harvest palmyra palm?

Pick fruit that comes away easily, or gather freshly fallen fruit. Don't break branches, and leave plenty for birds and animals.

How do you eat palmyra palm?

Nungu fresh. Boiled tubers (panang kizhangu) in Dec–Jan.

How do you store palmyra palm?

Dry the boiled tubers and grind to flour.

What is palmyra palm good for?

Calories.

What can you cook with palmyra palm?

  • Nungu drink: Peel and chop tender nungu, then mix with its own juice, chilled milk or water, and a little rose syrup or sugar.
  • Neera (fresh palm sap): Collected at dawn from tapped palms and drunk straight away.
