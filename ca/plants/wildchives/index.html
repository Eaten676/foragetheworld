Wild chives

Allium schoenoprasum
Also called Wild chives, Ciboulette sauvage

Wild chives is a wild green you can forage from May to July across Canada. Clumps of thin, hollow onion-scented leaves and round pink-purple flower heads on lake shores, river gravel and wet meadows.

Caution. Always smell for onion: death camas has flat, grass-like leaves, no onion smell, and is deadly.

When is wild chives in season in Canada?

Wild chives is usually in season May to July. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does wild chives grow in Canada?

Wild chives grows across Canada. Regions: British Columbia, Prairies, Ontario, Quebec, Atlantic Canada, The North.

How do you recognise wild chives?

Clumps of thin, hollow onion-scented leaves and round pink-purple flower heads on lake shores, river gravel and wet meadows.

How do you harvest wild chives?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat wild chives?

Snip over eggs, potatoes and fish.

How do you store wild chives?

Freeze chopped.

What can be confused with wild chives?

Death camas (Toxicoscordion venenosum), rated deadly. Camas has blue-violet star flowers. Death camas has creamy-white flowers in a tighter cluster. The bulbs and leaves look alike out of flower, so only dig plants marked while flowering.

What is wild chives good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with wild chives?

  • Wild chive butter: Chopped wild chives beaten into soft butter.
