Mahua

Madhuca longifolia
Also called Mahua, Ippe, Iluppai, Moha

Mahua is a edible flower or tea you can forage from March to June in Central forests, East, West coast and Deccan and Northern plains. Central India's tribal food tree. Fleshy, sweet cream flowers fall at night in spring. Seeds give oil.

When is mahua in season in India?

Mahua is usually in season March to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does mahua grow in India?

Mahua grows in Bihar, Chandigarh, Chhattisgarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Delhi, Goa, Gujarat, Haryana, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Punjab, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, West Bengal. Regions: Central forests, East, West coast and Deccan, Northern plains.

How do you recognise mahua?

Central India's tribal food tree. Fleshy, sweet cream flowers fall at night in spring. Seeds give oil.

How do you harvest mahua?

Sweep up freshly fallen flowers at dawn on clean ground. Never burn leaf litter to clear it, which is a major cause of forest fires.

How do you eat mahua?

Flowers cooked with rice or roasted; laddoos. Seed oil for cooking.

How do you store mahua?

Sun-dry the flowers. They keep about a year.

What is mahua good for?

Calories, Fat.

What can you cook with mahua?

  • Mahua bhat: Dried mahua flowers cooked into rice for natural sweetness. A tribal staple.
  • Mahua laddoo: Dried mahua flowers roasted and pounded with roasted grains and sesame.
