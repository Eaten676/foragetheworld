Tawa berries

Beilschmiedia tawa
Also called Tawa berries

Tawa berries is a wild fruit you can forage from February to April in Northland and Auckland, Central North Island, East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū and Top of the South. Forest tree with willow-like leaves and plum-coloured fruit with a large kernel.

Caution. Kernels must be thoroughly steamed before eating.

When is tawa berries in season in New Zealand?

Tawa berries is usually in season February to April. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does tawa berries grow in New Zealand?

Tawa berries grows in Auckland, Bay of Plenty, Gisborne, Hawke's Bay, Manawatū-Whanganui, Marlborough, Nelson, Northland, Taranaki, Tasman, Waikato, Wellington. Regions: Northland and Auckland, Central North Island, East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū, Top of the South.

How do you recognise tawa berries?

Forest tree with willow-like leaves and plum-coloured fruit with a large kernel.

How do you harvest tawa berries?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat tawa berries?

Flesh eaten fresh; kernels steamed for a long time in a hāngī.

How do you store tawa berries?

Dry steamed kernels.

What is tawa berries good for?

Calories.
