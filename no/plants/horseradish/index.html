Horseradish

Armoracia rusticana
Also called Pepperrot

Horseradish is a root or shoot you can forage from September to November in Eastern Norway and Southern Norway. Big clumps of long, wavy-edged, dock-like leaves on roadsides and field edges; the root is fiercely hot when grated.

Caution. Dig only with the landowner's permission. Its leaves look like dock; foxglove leaves are hairy and poisonous.

When is horseradish in season in Norway?

Horseradish is usually in season September to November. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does horseradish grow in Norway?

Horseradish grows in Agder, Akershus, Buskerud, Innlandet, Oslo, Rogaland, Telemark, Vestfold, Østfold. Regions: Eastern Norway, Southern Norway.

How do you recognise horseradish?

Big clumps of long, wavy-edged, dock-like leaves on roadsides and field edges; the root is fiercely hot when grated.

How do you harvest horseradish?

Only dig roots where you have the landowner's permission, since uprooting wild plants is illegal in many places. Take a few from a large patch and backfill the hole.

How do you eat horseradish?

Grated root in horseradish sauce, with beetroot or smoked fish.

How do you store horseradish?

Store roots in sand.

What is horseradish good for?

Vitamin C.
