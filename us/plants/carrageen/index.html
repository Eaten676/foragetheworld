Irish moss

Chondrus crispus
Also called Irish moss, Carrageen

Irish moss is a wild green you can forage from May to September in Northeast. Small, fan-shaped, dark red to purple seaweed tufts on low-tide rocks; bleaches to cream when dried.

Caution. Cut with scissors, leaving the holdfast on the rock.

When is irish moss in season in the United States?

Irish moss is usually in season May to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does irish moss grow in the United States?

Irish moss grows in Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia. Regions: Northeast.

How do you recognise irish moss?

Small, fan-shaped, dark red to purple seaweed tufts on low-tide rocks; bleaches to cream when dried.

How do you harvest irish moss?

Cut fronds at spring low tides, leaving the base, and bleach them by rinsing in rain and sun.

How do you eat irish moss?

Set into carrageen moss pudding with milk; thickens soups.

How do you store irish moss?

Dry and bleach in the sun.

What is irish moss good for?

Iron & calcium.

What can you cook with irish moss?

  • Irish moss blancmange: Irish moss simmered in milk with vanilla and set, a Maine classic.
