Hen of the woods

Grifola frondosa
Also called Hen of the woods, Maitake

Hen of the woods is a wild mushroom you can forage from September to October in Northeast, Southeast and Midwest. Large rosette of grey-brown overlapping caps at the base of old oaks in autumn, with pores (not gills) underneath.

Caution. Intermediate mushroom: learn it in person with an experienced forager before eating, and always cook it.

When is hen of the woods in season in the United States?

Hen of the woods is usually in season September to October. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does hen of the woods grow in the United States?

Hen of the woods grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest.

How do you recognise hen of the woods?

Large rosette of grey-brown overlapping caps at the base of old oaks in autumn, with pores (not gills) underneath.

How do you harvest hen of the woods?

Twist or cut mushrooms at the base, brush off soil in the woods and carry them in a basket, never a plastic bag. Only take ones you're 100% sure of, leave old and very young ones, and follow local limits.

How do you eat hen of the woods?

Roasted, sautéed or in soups. One cluster can weigh several kilos.

How do you store hen of the woods?

Cook and freeze.

What is hen of the woods good for?

Protein.

What can you cook with hen of the woods?

  • Roasted hen of the woods: Maitake clusters roasted until crisp at the edges.
