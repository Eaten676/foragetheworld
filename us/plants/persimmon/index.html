American persimmon

Diospyros virginiana
Also called Persimmon, Possumwood

American persimmon is a wild fruit you can forage from September to November in Southeast, Midwest, Northeast and Great Plains. Tree with blocky, alligator-skin bark; orange fruit that must be fully soft and wrinkly or it puckers the mouth.

When is American persimmon in season in the United States?

American persimmon is usually in season September to November. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does American persimmon grow in the United States?

American persimmon grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Southeast, Midwest, Northeast, Great Plains.

How do you recognise American persimmon?

Tree with blocky, alligator-skin bark; orange fruit that must be fully soft and wrinkly or it puckers the mouth.

How do you harvest American persimmon?

Gather fruit from the ground once soft and wrinkled, after the first frost.

How do you eat American persimmon?

Persimmon pudding and bread.

How do you store American persimmon?

Freeze the pulp.

What is American persimmon good for?

Calories, Vitamin C, Vitamin A.

What can you cook with American persimmon?

  • Persimmon pudding: Spiced baked pudding of ripe persimmon pulp, a Midwestern and Southern tradition.
