Wood nettle

Laportea canadensis
Also called Wood nettle

Wood nettle is a wild green you can forage from April to June in Northeast, Midwest and Southeast. Stinging plant of rich, moist woods with broad, alternate leaves; tastier and more tender than stinging nettle.

Caution. Wear gloves; cooking removes the sting.

When is wood nettle in season in the United States?

Wood nettle is usually in season April to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does wood nettle grow in the United States?

Wood nettle grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Midwest, Southeast.

How do you recognise wood nettle?

Stinging plant of rich, moist woods with broad, alternate leaves; tastier and more tender than stinging nettle.

How do you harvest wood nettle?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat wood nettle?

Use like nettle: blanched, then in soups, pesto and pasta.

How do you store wood nettle?

Blanch and freeze.

What is wood nettle good for?

Protein, Iron & calcium.

What can you cook with wood nettle?

  • Wood nettle pesto pasta: Blanched wood nettle blended into pesto and tossed with pasta.
