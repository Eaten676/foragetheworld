Lamb's quarters

Chenopodium album
Also called Lamb's quarters, Chénopode blanc

Lamb's quarters is a wild green you can forage from June to October across Canada. Goose-foot-shaped leaves with a white mealy bloom on the young tips; weed of gardens and field edges.

Caution. Contains oxalates; cook it and eat in moderation.

When is lamb's quarters in season in Canada?

Lamb's quarters is usually in season June to October. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does lamb's quarters grow in Canada?

Lamb's quarters grows across Canada. Regions: British Columbia, Prairies, Ontario, Quebec, Atlantic Canada, The North.

How do you recognise lamb's quarters?

Goose-foot-shaped leaves with a white mealy bloom on the young tips; weed of gardens and field edges.

How do you harvest lamb's quarters?

Pick the top 10–15 cm of young plants; strip seeds in autumn by running a hand up the dry spikes.

How do you eat lamb's quarters?

Use like spinach: sautéed, in quiche or saag. Seeds later in the year.

How do you store lamb's quarters?

Blanch and freeze.

What is lamb's quarters good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C, Iron & calcium.
