Wild asparagus

Asparagus officinalis
Also called Wild asparagus

Wild asparagus is a root or shoot you can forage from April to June in Northeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest and California. Escaped spears along ditches and fence lines; easiest found by last year's tall, feathery dead stalks.

When is wild asparagus in season in the United States?

Wild asparagus is usually in season April to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does wild asparagus grow in the United States?

Wild asparagus grows in California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Hawaii, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Dakota, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming. Regions: Northeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest, California.

How do you recognise wild asparagus?

Escaped spears along ditches and fence lines; easiest found by last year's tall, feathery dead stalks.

How do you harvest wild asparagus?

Only dig roots where you have the landowner's permission, since uprooting wild plants is illegal in many places. Take a few from a large patch and backfill the hole.

How do you eat wild asparagus?

Grill, steam or roast like garden asparagus.

How do you store wild asparagus?

Blanch and freeze, or pickle.

What is wild asparagus good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C.
