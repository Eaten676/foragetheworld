Dewberry

Rubus flagellaris / R. trivialis
Also called Dewberry

Dewberry is a wild fruit you can forage from May to July in Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains and Southwest. Trailing blackberry that creeps along the ground in fields and roadsides; fruit ripens weeks before upright blackberries.

When is dewberry in season in the United States?

Dewberry is usually in season May to July. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does dewberry grow in the United States?

Dewberry grows in Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, Nevada, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Southwest.

How do you recognise dewberry?

Trailing blackberry that creeps along the ground in fields and roadsides; fruit ripens weeks before upright blackberries.

How do you harvest dewberry?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat dewberry?

Cobbler, jam and pie, used like blackberries.

How do you store dewberry?

Freeze.

What is dewberry good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with dewberry?

  • Dewberry cobbler: Early dewberries under a buttery cobbler crust.
