Karengo

Pyropia
Also called Karengo, Nori

Karengo is a wild green you can forage from July to November in East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū, Canterbury, Otago and Southland, West Coast and Top of the South. Thin, glossy purple-black seaweed sheets on exposed rocks in winter and spring.

Caution. Collect only from clean coasts and follow local rāhui.

When is karengo in season in New Zealand?

Karengo is usually in season July to November. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does karengo grow in New Zealand?

Karengo grows in Canterbury, Gisborne, Hawke's Bay, Manawatū-Whanganui, Marlborough, Nelson, Otago, Southland, Taranaki, Tasman, Wellington, West Coast. Regions: East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū, Canterbury, Otago and Southland, West Coast, Top of the South.

How do you recognise karengo?

Thin, glossy purple-black seaweed sheets on exposed rocks in winter and spring.

How do you harvest karengo?

Pluck sheets off rocks at low tide in winter and spring, taking a little from many rocks.

How do you eat karengo?

Dried and toasted, in broths and fritters.

How do you store karengo?

Dry.

What is karengo good for?

Iron & calcium, Protein, Vitamin A.

What can you cook with karengo?

  • Karengo broth: Seaweed simmered into a savoury broth with fish.
