Elderberry

Sambucus nigra
Also called Svarthyll

Elderberry is a wild fruit you can forage from August to September in Southern Norway and Western Norway. Shrub with corky bark and flat drooping umbrellas of tiny black berries on red stalks.

Caution. Always cook. Raw berries, stems and leaves cause nausea.

When is elderberry in season in Norway?

Elderberry is usually in season August to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does elderberry grow in Norway?

Elderberry grows in Agder, Møre og Romsdal, Rogaland, Telemark, Vestfold, Vestland. Regions: Southern Norway, Western Norway.

How do you recognise elderberry?

Shrub with corky bark and flat drooping umbrellas of tiny black berries on red stalks.

How do you harvest elderberry?

Cut whole drooping clusters when every berry is dark purple; strip off stalks with a fork before cooking.

How do you eat elderberry?

Cooked: syrup, soup, jelly, chutney.

How do you store elderberry?

Freeze on the stem, then fork off.

What can be confused with elderberry?

Hemlock (Conium maculatum), rated deadly. Elder is a woody shrub; hemlock is a soft plant with hollow stems. Hemlock stems are smooth with purple blotches and smell mousy. Ground elder has leaves in threes on a triangular, unspotted stem.

What is elderberry good for?

Vitamin C, Vitamin A.
