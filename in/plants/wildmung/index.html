Wild mung

Vigna radiata
Also called Jangli moong, Kaadu hesaru

Wild mung is a wild seed or grain you can forage from October to December in South India, Central forests, East and West coast and Deccan. Wild form of green gram, trailing over scrub and field bunds, with small hairy black pods. Smaller seeds than the farmed kind.

When is wild mung in season in India?

Wild mung is usually in season October to December. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does wild mung grow in India?

Wild mung grows in Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Goa, Gujarat, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Kerala, Lakshadweep, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Puducherry, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, West Bengal. Regions: South India, Central forests, East, West coast and Deccan.

How do you recognise wild mung?

Wild form of green gram, trailing over scrub and field bunds, with small hairy black pods. Smaller seeds than the farmed kind.

How do you harvest wild mung?

Wait until seed heads or pods are dry and ripe, then strip or shake them into a bag or onto a cloth before they shatter. Thresh, winnow and sun-dry the seeds well.

How do you eat wild mung?

Dal, sprouts, or boiled with salt.

How do you store wild mung?

Dry the pods, thresh, and store the seeds. Keeps a year.

What is wild mung good for?

Protein, Iron & calcium.

What can you cook with wild mung?

  • Wild mung dal: Small wild green gram cooked into a simple dal with garlic and cumin tempering.
