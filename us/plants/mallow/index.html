Common mallow

Malva neglecta
Also called Cheeseweed, Common mallow

Common mallow is a wild green you can forage from April to September across the United States. Round, scalloped leaves on long stalks and pale pink five-petalled flowers; seeds look like tiny wheels of cheese.

When is common mallow in season in the United States?

Common mallow is usually in season April to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does common mallow grow in the United States?

Common mallow grows across the United States. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Southwest, Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest, California, Alaska.

How do you recognise common mallow?

Round, scalloped leaves on long stalks and pale pink five-petalled flowers; seeds look like tiny wheels of cheese.

How do you harvest common mallow?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat common mallow?

Leaves in soups to thicken them; green seeds as a nibble.

How do you store common mallow?

Dry leaves.

What is common mallow good for?

Vitamin A.
