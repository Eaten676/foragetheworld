Staghorn sumac

Rhus typhina
Also called Staghorn sumac

Staghorn sumac is a wild fruit you can forage from August to September across the United States. Shrub with velvety branches and upright cone-shaped clusters of fuzzy red berries. Sour.

Caution. Poison sumac has white berries and grows in swamps; red, upright clusters are safe.

When is staghorn sumac in season in the United States?

Staghorn sumac is usually in season August to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does staghorn sumac grow in the United States?

Staghorn sumac grows across the United States. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Southwest, Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest, California, Alaska.

How do you recognise staghorn sumac?

Shrub with velvety branches and upright cone-shaped clusters of fuzzy red berries. Sour.

How do you harvest staghorn sumac?

Cut whole red clusters on a dry day before rain washes off the sour coating.

How do you eat staghorn sumac?

Sumac-ade (cold-steeped lemonade) or ground as a spice.

How do you store staghorn sumac?

Dry whole clusters.

What is staghorn sumac good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with staghorn sumac?

  • Sumac lemonade: Rub sumac clusters in cold water for an hour, strain through a cloth and sweeten. Pink and tart.
