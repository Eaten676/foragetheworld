Wild black currant

Ribes americanum / R. hudsonianum
Also called Wild black currant, American black currant

Wild black currant is a wild fruit you can forage from July to August in Northeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Rocky Mountains, Alaska and Pacific Northwest. Thornless shrub of wet thickets with maple-like leaves dotted with yellow resin glands underneath and drooping strings of black berries.

When is wild black currant in season in the United States?

Wild black currant is usually in season July to August. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does wild black currant grow in the United States?

Wild black currant grows in Alaska, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Dakota, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming. Regions: Northeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Rocky Mountains, Alaska, Pacific Northwest.

How do you recognise wild black currant?

Thornless shrub of wet thickets with maple-like leaves dotted with yellow resin glands underneath and drooping strings of black berries.

How do you harvest wild black currant?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat wild black currant?

Jam, jelly, syrup and pemmican.

How do you store wild black currant?

Freeze or make jam.

What is wild black currant good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with wild black currant?

  • Wild black currant jam: Wild currants cooked with sugar into a tart, rich jam.
