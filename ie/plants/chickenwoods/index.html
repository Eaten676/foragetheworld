Chicken of the woods

Laetiporus sulphureus
Also called Chicken of the woods

Chicken of the woods is a wild mushroom you can forage from May to September across Ireland. Shelves of bright sulphur-yellow and orange brackets on oaks, willows and cherry trees; soft and moist when young, with tiny pores underneath.

Caution. Always cook well; some people react, so try a little first. Never pick from yew, eucalyptus or conifers, which can make it toxic.

When is chicken of the woods in season in Ireland?

Chicken of the woods is usually in season May to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does chicken of the woods grow in Ireland?

Chicken of the woods grows across Ireland. Regions: Munster, Leinster, Connacht, Ulster (Republic).

How do you recognise chicken of the woods?

Shelves of bright sulphur-yellow and orange brackets on oaks, willows and cherry trees; soft and moist when young, with tiny pores underneath.

How do you harvest chicken of the woods?

Cut the soft outer edges of young brackets with a knife, leaving the tough base on the tree.

How do you eat chicken of the woods?

Cooked thoroughly: tastes and shreds like chicken.

How do you store chicken of the woods?

Cook, then freeze.

What is chicken of the woods good for?

Protein.

What can you cook with chicken of the woods?

  • Chicken of the woods stew: The mushroom braised with onions, stout and thyme.
