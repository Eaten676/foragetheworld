Soapberry

Shepherdia canadensis
Also called Soapberry, Russet buffaloberry, Hooshum

Soapberry is a wild fruit you can forage from July to August in Alaska, Pacific Northwest, Rocky Mountains, Midwest and Northeast. Thornless shrub with oval leaves, green on top and rusty-dotted below; small translucent red berries.

Caution. Bitter and soapy if eaten in quantity; use a few spoonfuls and whip in a clean, grease-free bowl.

When is soapberry in season in the United States?

Soapberry is usually in season July to August. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does soapberry grow in the United States?

Soapberry grows in Alaska, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, Ohio, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming. Regions: Alaska, Pacific Northwest, Rocky Mountains, Midwest, Northeast.

How do you recognise soapberry?

Thornless shrub with oval leaves, green on top and rusty-dotted below; small translucent red berries.

How do you harvest soapberry?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat soapberry?

Whipped with water and a little sugar into a pink foam ('Indian ice cream', sxusem).

How do you store soapberry?

Freeze or dry into cakes.

What is soapberry good for?

Vitamin C.
