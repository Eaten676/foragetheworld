Sassafras

Sassafras albidum
Also called Sassafras, Filé

Sassafras is a edible flower or tea you can forage from May to September in Northeast, Southeast and Midwest. Eastern tree with three leaf shapes on one branch (mitten, ghost and oval) and green twigs that smell of root beer.

Caution. Root bark contains safrole: use leaves, occasionally, and avoid in pregnancy.

When is sassafras in season in the United States?

Sassafras is usually in season May to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does sassafras grow in the United States?

Sassafras grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest.

How do you recognise sassafras?

Eastern tree with three leaf shapes on one branch (mitten, ghost and oval) and green twigs that smell of root beer.

How do you harvest sassafras?

Pick flowers on a dry, sunny morning once the dew has gone, when the scent is strongest. Take a few heads from each plant so it can still set fruit.

How do you eat sassafras?

Dried, ground leaves are filé powder for gumbo; twigs for a light tea.

How do you store sassafras?

Dry leaves.

What can you cook with sassafras?

  • Gumbo with filé: Chicken and sausage gumbo thickened at the table with filé powder.
