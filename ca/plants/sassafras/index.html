Sassafras

Sassafras albidum
Also called Sassafras, Sassafras officinal

Sassafras is a edible flower or tea you can forage from June to September in Ontario. Eastern tree with three leaf shapes on one branch (mitten, ghost and oval) and green twigs that smell of root beer.

Caution. Root bark contains safrole: use leaves, occasionally, and avoid in pregnancy.

When is sassafras in season in Canada?

Sassafras is usually in season June to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does sassafras grow in Canada?

Sassafras grows in Ontario. Regions: Ontario.

How do you recognise sassafras?

Eastern tree with three leaf shapes on one branch (mitten, ghost and oval) and green twigs that smell of root beer.

How do you harvest sassafras?

Pick flowers on a dry, sunny morning once the dew has gone, when the scent is strongest. Take a few heads from each plant so it can still set fruit.

How do you eat sassafras?

Dried, ground leaves are filé powder for gumbo; twigs for a light tea.

How do you store sassafras?

Dry leaves.
