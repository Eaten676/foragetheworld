Fireweed

Chamerion angustifolium
Also called Fireweed

Fireweed is a wild green you can forage from May to August in Alaska, Pacific Northwest, Rocky Mountains, Northeast and Midwest. Tall plant of burns and clearings with spikes of magenta flowers; young shoots in spring.

When is fireweed in season in the United States?

Fireweed is usually in season May to August. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does fireweed grow in the United States?

Fireweed grows in Alaska, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, Ohio, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming. Regions: Alaska, Pacific Northwest, Rocky Mountains, Northeast, Midwest.

How do you recognise fireweed?

Tall plant of burns and clearings with spikes of magenta flowers; young shoots in spring.

How do you harvest fireweed?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat fireweed?

Shoots like asparagus; flowers in jelly; leaves as tea.

How do you store fireweed?

Dry leaves.

What is fireweed good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C.

What can you cook with fireweed?

  • Fireweed tea: Fermented fireweed leaves brewed as tea.
