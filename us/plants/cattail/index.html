Cattail

Typha latifolia
Also called Cattail, Bulrush

Cattail is a root or shoot you can forage from April to July across the United States. Tall flat leaves in marshes with brown sausage-shaped seed heads. Young inner shoots taste like cucumber.

Caution. Only from clean water. Young iris leaves look similar but are poisonous and don't have the cattail's fluffy head.

When is cattail in season in the United States?

Cattail is usually in season April to July. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does cattail grow in the United States?

Cattail grows across the United States. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Southwest, Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest, California, Alaska.

How do you recognise cattail?

Tall flat leaves in marshes with brown sausage-shaped seed heads. Young inner shoots taste like cucumber.

How do you harvest cattail?

Pull young inner shoots in spring; green flower spikes in early summer; yellow pollen by shaking heads into a bag.

How do you eat cattail?

Peel shoots and eat raw or sautéed; green flower spikes boiled like corn; yellow pollen as flour.

How do you store cattail?

Dry pollen.

What can be confused with cattail?

Yellow flag iris (Iris pseudacorus), rated harmful. Cattail leaves are D-shaped in section and spongy; iris leaves are flat and sharp-edged in a fan. Iris has yellow flowers; cattail has a brown sausage head.

What is cattail good for?

Calories.
