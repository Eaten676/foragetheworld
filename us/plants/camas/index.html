Camas

Camassia quamash
Also called Camas, Quamash

Camas is a root or shoot you can forage from June to September in Pacific Northwest and Rocky Mountains. Meadow lily with grass-like leaves and spikes of blue-violet star flowers.

Caution. Death camas grows alongside and kills. Mark plants in flower and dig only blue ones.

When is camas in season in the United States?

Camas is usually in season June to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does camas grow in the United States?

Camas grows in Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Oregon, Utah, Washington, Wyoming. Regions: Pacific Northwest, Rocky Mountains.

How do you recognise camas?

Meadow lily with grass-like leaves and spikes of blue-violet star flowers.

How do you harvest camas?

Only dig roots where you have the landowner's permission, since uprooting wild plants is illegal in many places. Take a few from a large patch and backfill the hole.

How do you eat camas?

Slow-roasted or pit-cooked until sweet and dark.

How do you store camas?

Dry cooked bulbs.

What can be confused with camas?

Death camas (Toxicoscordion venenosum), rated deadly. Camas has blue-violet star flowers. Death camas has creamy-white flowers in a tighter cluster. The bulbs and leaves look alike out of flower, so only dig plants marked while flowering.

What is camas good for?

Calories.

What can you cook with camas?

  • Camas and salmon bake: Slow-baked camas bulbs alongside cedar-planked salmon, after Pacific Northwest tradition.
