Morel

Morchella spp.
Also called Morkel

Morel is a wild mushroom you can forage from May to June in Eastern Norway and Southern Norway. Honeycomb-capped spring mushroom of old orchards, riverbanks, gardens and burned ground. Cut in half: a true morel is hollow from tip to base.

Caution. Intermediate mushroom: learn it in person first. False morels are brain-like, stuffed with cottony fibres, and can kill.

When is morel in season in Norway?

Morel is usually in season May to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does morel grow in Norway?

Morel grows in Agder, Akershus, Buskerud, Innlandet, Oslo, Rogaland, Telemark, Vestfold, Østfold. Regions: Eastern Norway, Southern Norway.

How do you recognise morel?

Honeycomb-capped spring mushroom of old orchards, riverbanks, gardens and burned ground. Cut in half: a true morel is hollow from tip to base.

How do you harvest morel?

Twist or cut mushrooms at the base, brush off soil in the woods and carry them in a basket, never a plastic bag. Only take ones you're 100% sure of, leave old and very young ones, and follow local limits.

How do you eat morel?

Sautéed in butter or in cream sauce. Always cook.

How do you store morel?

Dry on a string.

What can be confused with morel?

False morel (Gyromitra esculenta), rated deadly. Slice it in half: a true morel is one continuous hollow chamber. False morels are brain-like and wrinkled, not pitted, and stuffed with cottony fibres.

What is morel good for?

Protein, Iron & calcium.
