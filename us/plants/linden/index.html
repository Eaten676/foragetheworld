Linden flowers

Tilia americana
Also called Basswood, Linden

Linden flowers is a edible flower or tea you can forage from June to July in Northeast, Southeast, Midwest and Great Plains. Big tree with heart-shaped leaves; dangling clusters of pale yellow honey-scented flowers under a leafy wing.

When is linden flowers in season in the United States?

Linden flowers is usually in season June to July. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does linden flowers grow in the United States?

Linden flowers grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains.

How do you recognise linden flowers?

Big tree with heart-shaped leaves; dangling clusters of pale yellow honey-scented flowers under a leafy wing.

How do you harvest linden flowers?

Pick flower clusters with their leafy bract when the scent is strongest, early in flowering.

How do you eat linden flowers?

Calming tea.

How do you store linden flowers?

Dry flowers with their bract.
