Stinging nettle

Urtica dioica
Also called Bichhu buti, Kandali, Sisnu

Stinging nettle is a wild green you can forage from February to November in Himalaya. Hairy toothed leaves on stalks near Himalayan villages and streams.

Caution. Wear gloves to pick it. Blanching removes the sting.

When is stinging nettle in season in India?

Stinging nettle is usually in season February to November. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does stinging nettle grow in India?

Stinging nettle grows in Arunachal Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Sikkim, Uttarakhand. Regions: Himalaya.

How do you recognise stinging nettle?

Hairy toothed leaves on stalks near Himalayan villages and streams.

How do you harvest stinging nettle?

Wear gloves and long sleeves. Snip only the top four to six leaves of young plants.

How do you eat stinging nettle?

Kandali saag and soups once blanched.

How do you store stinging nettle?

Blanch, then shade-dry and powder.

What is stinging nettle good for?

Protein, Iron & calcium.

What can you cook with stinging nettle?

  • Nettle tea: Steep fresh (gloved) or dried nettle leaves in hot water for 5–10 minutes.
  • Bathua saag: Bathua slow-cooked with mustard greens, garlic and makki atta, finished with ghee.
  • Kandali saag: Blanched nettles cooked with garlic, chilli and a little rice flour. A Kumaoni winter dish.
