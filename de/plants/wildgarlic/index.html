Wild garlic

Allium ursinum
Also called Bärlauch

Wild garlic is a wild green you can forage from March to May in South and the Alps, West and East. Carpets of broad, soft, bright green leaves in damp spring woods, smelling strongly of garlic; white star flowers in May.

Caution. Always crush a leaf and smell garlic. Lily of the valley, autumn crocus and lords-and-ladies grow in the same woods and are poisonous.

When is wild garlic in season in Germany?

Wild garlic is usually in season March to May. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does wild garlic grow in Germany?

Wild garlic grows in Baden-Württemberg, Bavaria, Berlin, Brandenburg, Hesse, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, North Rhine-Westphalia, Rhineland-Palatinate, Saarland, Saxony, Saxony-Anhalt, Thuringia. Regions: South and the Alps, West, East.

How do you recognise wild garlic?

Carpets of broad, soft, bright green leaves in damp spring woods, smelling strongly of garlic; white star flowers in May.

How do you harvest wild garlic?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat wild garlic?

Pesto, soup, butter, in omelettes; flowers and green seeds too.

How do you store wild garlic?

Freeze as pesto or garlic butter.

What can be confused with wild garlic?

Lily of the valley (Convallaria majalis), rated deadly. Wild garlic leaves smell strongly of garlic when crushed. Lily of the valley smells of nothing. Wild garlic leaves grow singly on their own stalk; lily of the valley comes in pairs wrapped around one stem. Wash your hands between leaves, or you'll smell garlic on everything. Autumn crocus (Colchicum autumnale), rated deadly. Autumn crocus leaves are thick, stiff and glossy in a tight bunch, with no garlic smell. They appear in spring in damp meadows, which is also where wild garlic grows near woods. Lords-and-ladies (Arum maculatum), rated poisonous. Arum leaves are arrow-shaped with a network of veins, often dark-spotted. Wild garlic has parallel veins and smells of garlic.

What is wild garlic good for?

Vitamin C, Vitamin A.

What can you cook with wild garlic?

  • Wild garlic soup (Bärlauchsuppe): Creamy potato soup blended with wild garlic.
  • Wild garlic pesto (Bärlauchpesto): Wild garlic, hard cheese, nuts and oil.
  • Wild garlic Maultaschen: Swabian pasta pockets filled with wild garlic and meat or cheese.
