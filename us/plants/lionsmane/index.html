Lion's mane

Hericium spp.
Also called Lion's mane

Lion's mane is a wild mushroom you can forage from August to November across the United States. White, shaggy ball of hanging icicle-like spines on wounds in hardwood trees; no gills, no cap.

Caution. Intermediate mushroom: learn it in person with an experienced forager before eating, and always cook it.

When is lion's mane in season in the United States?

Lion's mane is usually in season August to November. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does lion's mane grow in the United States?

Lion's mane grows across the United States. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Southwest, Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest, California, Alaska.

How do you recognise lion's mane?

White, shaggy ball of hanging icicle-like spines on wounds in hardwood trees; no gills, no cap.

How do you harvest lion's mane?

Twist or cut mushrooms at the base, brush off soil in the woods and carry them in a basket, never a plastic bag. Only take ones you're 100% sure of, leave old and very young ones, and follow local limits.

How do you eat lion's mane?

Sliced and seared in butter; tastes like crab or lobster.

How do you store lion's mane?

Cook and freeze.

What is lion's mane good for?

Protein.

What can you cook with lion's mane?

  • Lion's mane crab cakes: Shredded lion's mane in place of crab, pan-fried.
