Marsh samphire

Salicornia europaea
Also called Zeekraal

Marsh samphire is a wild green you can forage from June to August in Coast, dunes and Wadden. Fleshy, jointed, leafless green stems like tiny cacti on tidal mud flats. Salty and crunchy.

Caution. Cut, don't uproot, and check whether picking is allowed on that marsh; many are nature reserves.

When is marsh samphire in season in the Netherlands?

Marsh samphire is usually in season June to August. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does marsh samphire grow in the Netherlands?

Marsh samphire grows in Friesland, Groningen, North Holland, South Holland, Zeeland. Regions: Coast, dunes and Wadden.

How do you recognise marsh samphire?

Fleshy, jointed, leafless green stems like tiny cacti on tidal mud flats. Salty and crunchy.

How do you harvest marsh samphire?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat marsh samphire?

Steamed with butter alongside fish, or pickled.

How do you store marsh samphire?

Pickle.

What is marsh samphire good for?

Vitamin A, Iron & calcium.

What can you cook with marsh samphire?

  • Fried samphire (zeekraal): Samphire tossed in butter with garlic.
