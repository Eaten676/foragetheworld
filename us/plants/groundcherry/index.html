Ground cherry

Physalis heterophylla
Also called Ground cherry

Ground cherry is a wild fruit you can forage from July to September in Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains and Great Plains. Low plant with yellow bell flowers; berries inside papery lanterns ripen golden when the husk dries.

Caution. Only eat fully ripe, yellow fruit; unripe fruit and leaves are toxic.

When is ground cherry in season in the United States?

Ground cherry is usually in season July to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does ground cherry grow in the United States?

Ground cherry grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Great Plains.

How do you recognise ground cherry?

Low plant with yellow bell flowers; berries inside papery lanterns ripen golden when the husk dries.

How do you harvest ground cherry?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat ground cherry?

Fresh, jam, salsa.

How do you store ground cherry?

Store in husks.

What is ground cherry good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with ground cherry?

  • Ground cherry salsa: Ripe ground cherries with chilli, onion and lime.
