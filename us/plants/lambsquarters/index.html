Lamb's quarters

Chenopodium album
Also called Lamb's quarters, Wild spinach, Goosefoot

Lamb's quarters is a wild green you can forage from May to September across the United States. Goose-foot-shaped leaves with a white mealy bloom on the young tips; weed of gardens and field edges.

Caution. Contains oxalates; cook it and eat in moderation.

When is lamb's quarters in season in the United States?

Lamb's quarters is usually in season May to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does lamb's quarters grow in the United States?

Lamb's quarters grows across the United States. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Southwest, Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest, California, Alaska.

How do you recognise lamb's quarters?

Goose-foot-shaped leaves with a white mealy bloom on the young tips; weed of gardens and field edges.

How do you harvest lamb's quarters?

Pick the top 10–15 cm of young plants; strip seeds in autumn by running a hand up the dry spikes.

How do you eat lamb's quarters?

Use like spinach: sautéed, in quiche or saag. Seeds later in the year.

How do you store lamb's quarters?

Blanch and freeze.

What is lamb's quarters good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C, Iron & calcium.

What can you cook with lamb's quarters?

  • Lamb's quarters spanakopita: Wild greens with feta in crisp filo.
