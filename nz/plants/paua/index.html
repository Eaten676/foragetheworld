Pāua

Haliotis iris
Also called Pāua

Pāua is a wild protein you can forage all year round across New Zealand. Abalone clinging to subtidal rocks, with an iridescent shell.

Caution. Strict limits: 125 mm minimum size, daily bag limits, no scuba. Check MPI rules.

When is pāua in season in New Zealand?

Pāua is usually in season all year round. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does pāua grow in New Zealand?

Pāua grows across New Zealand. Regions: Northland and Auckland, Central North Island, East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū, Top of the South, West Coast, Canterbury, Otago and Southland.

How do you recognise pāua?

Abalone clinging to subtidal rocks, with an iridescent shell.

How do you harvest pāua?

Check local rules, licences, size limits and water-quality warnings before taking any shellfish or fish.

How do you eat pāua?

Minced into fritters, or sliced and seared.

How do you store pāua?

Eat fresh or freeze.

What is pāua good for?

Protein, Iron & calcium.

What can you cook with pāua?

  • Pāua fritters: Minced pāua in egg batter, fried.
