Agathi flowers & leaves

Sesbania grandiflora
Also called Agastya, Agase, Agathi keerai, Bok phool

Agathi flowers & leaves is a edible flower or tea you can forage from October to February in South India, East and West coast and Deccan. Slender tree with long hanging white or pink flowers. Leaves are a calcium-rich green.

When is agathi flowers & leaves in season in India?

Agathi flowers & leaves is usually in season October to February. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does agathi flowers & leaves grow in India?

Agathi flowers & leaves grows in Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Goa, Gujarat, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Kerala, Lakshadweep, Maharashtra, Odisha, Puducherry, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, West Bengal. Regions: South India, East, West coast and Deccan.

How do you recognise agathi flowers & leaves?

Slender tree with long hanging white or pink flowers. Leaves are a calcium-rich green.

How do you harvest agathi flowers & leaves?

Pick buds or petals, or gather fresh fallen flowers early in the morning. Leave enough on the plant to set fruit and seed.

How do you eat agathi flowers & leaves?

Agase hoovina bajji, keerai poriyal, bok phool bhaja.

How do you store agathi flowers & leaves?

Use fresh. Shade-dry the leaves.

What is agathi flowers & leaves good for?

Vitamin A, Iron & calcium.

What can you cook with agathi flowers & leaves?

  • Agase hoovina bajji: Agathi flowers dipped in spiced besan batter and fried crisp. A Karnataka monsoon snack.
