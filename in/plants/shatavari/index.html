Wild asparagus shoots

Asparagus racemosus
Also called Shatavari, Satmuli, Halavu makkala thayi

Wild asparagus shoots is a root or shoot you can forage from June to August in Himalaya, Central forests, Northern plains, South India, West coast and Deccan and East. Thorny climbing asparagus with needle-like leaves. Tender spear-like shoots come up in the monsoon.

Caution. Take only a few shoots from each plant. The roots are harvested for medicine and the plant is declining in the wild.

When is wild asparagus shoots in season in India?

Wild asparagus shoots is usually in season June to August. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does wild asparagus shoots grow in India?

Wild asparagus shoots grows in Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Andhra Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, Bihar, Chandigarh, Chhattisgarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Delhi, Goa, Gujarat, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Kerala, Ladakh, Lakshadweep, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Puducherry, Punjab, Sikkim, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, West Bengal. Regions: Himalaya, Central forests, Northern plains, South India, West coast and Deccan, East.

How do you recognise wild asparagus shoots?

Thorny climbing asparagus with needle-like leaves. Tender spear-like shoots come up in the monsoon.

How do you harvest wild asparagus shoots?

Snap young spears at 15–20 cm, and leave most of the plant's shoots to grow.

How do you eat wild asparagus shoots?

Young shoots stir-fried or as sabzi.

How do you store wild asparagus shoots?

Use fresh, or blanch and freeze.

What is wild asparagus shoots good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C.

What can you cook with wild asparagus shoots?

  • Shatavari stir-fry: Wild asparagus spears tossed in mustard oil with garlic.
