Elderberry

Sambucus canadensis
Also called American elder, Sureau

Elderberry is a wild fruit you can forage from August to September in Ontario, Quebec, Atlantic Canada and British Columbia. Shrub with leaves of 5–11 toothed leaflets and flat umbrellas of tiny black-purple berries in late summer.

Caution. Always cook. Raw berries, stems and leaves cause nausea. Red elderberry is more toxic.

When is elderberry in season in Canada?

Elderberry is usually in season August to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does elderberry grow in Canada?

Elderberry grows in British Columbia, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, Ontario, Prince Edward Island, Quebec. Regions: Ontario, Quebec, Atlantic Canada, British Columbia.

How do you recognise elderberry?

Shrub with leaves of 5–11 toothed leaflets and flat umbrellas of tiny black-purple berries in late summer.

How do you harvest elderberry?

Cut whole drooping clusters when every berry is dark purple; strip off stalks with a fork before cooking.

How do you eat elderberry?

Cooked into syrup, jelly or pie.

How do you store elderberry?

Freeze on the stem, then fork off.

What can be confused with elderberry?

Poison hemlock (Conium maculatum), rated deadly. Elder is a woody shrub. Poison hemlock is a soft, hollow-stemmed plant. Hemlock stems are smooth with purple blotches. Crushed hemlock smells mousy and unpleasant. Pokeweed berries (Phytolacca americana), rated poisonous. Pokeberries hang in long drooping strings on magenta stems. Elderberries sit in flat-topped umbrellas on a woody shrub.

What is elderberry good for?

Vitamin C, Vitamin A.
