St George's mushroom

Calocybe gambosa
Also called Mairitterling, Tricholome de la Saint-Georges, Prugnolo

St George's mushroom is a wild mushroom you can forage from April to June across Switzerland. Stout, creamy-white mushroom in rings in grassland and woodland edges around 23 April; crowded white gills and a strong mealy smell.

Caution. Intermediate mushroom: learn it in person first. Deadly fibrecaps and poisonous white mushrooms grow at the same time.

When is st george's mushroom in season in Switzerland?

St George's mushroom is usually in season April to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does st george's mushroom grow in Switzerland?

St George's mushroom grows across Switzerland. Regions: Jura, Swiss Plateau, Alps, Ticino and southern valleys.

How do you recognise st george's mushroom?

Stout, creamy-white mushroom in rings in grassland and woodland edges around 23 April; crowded white gills and a strong mealy smell.

How do you harvest st george's mushroom?

Twist or cut mushrooms at the base, brush off soil in the woods and carry them in a basket, never a plastic bag. Only take ones you're 100% sure of, leave old and very young ones, and follow local limits.

How do you eat st george's mushroom?

Fried in butter, with eggs, or in risotto.

How do you store st george's mushroom?

Cook and freeze.

What can be confused with st george's mushroom?

Deadly fibrecap (Inocybe erubescens), rated deadly. St George's mushroom has a strong mealy, cucumber-like smell and crowded white gills. The deadly fibrecap has a pointed, silky, radially split cap that bruises red, and fruits a little later in the same places.

What is st george's mushroom good for?

Protein.
