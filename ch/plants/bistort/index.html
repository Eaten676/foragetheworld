Bistort

Bistorta officinalis
Also called Schlangen-Knöterich, Renouée bistorte, Bistorta

Bistort is a wild green you can forage from March to May in Alps and Jura. Damp-meadow plant with long, dock-like leaves on winged stalks and pink bottle-brush flower spikes.

When is bistort in season in Switzerland?

Bistort is usually in season March to May. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does bistort grow in Switzerland?

Bistort grows in Aargau, Appenzell Ausserrhoden, Appenzell Innerrhoden, Basel-Landschaft, Bern, Fribourg, Glarus, Graubünden, Jura, Lucerne, Neuchâtel, Nidwalden, Obwalden, Schaffhausen, Schwyz, Solothurn, St. Gallen, Ticino, Uri, Valais, Vaud. Regions: Alps, Jura.

How do you recognise bistort?

Damp-meadow plant with long, dock-like leaves on winged stalks and pink bottle-brush flower spikes.

How do you harvest bistort?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat bistort?

Young leaves in Cumbria's Easter-ledger pudding with oats and nettles, or cooked like spinach.

How do you store bistort?

Blanch and freeze.

What is bistort good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C.
