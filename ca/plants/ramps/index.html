Ramps

Allium tricoccum
Also called Ramps, Ail des bois

Ramps is a wild green you can forage from May to June in Ontario and Quebec. Broad, smooth spring leaves in rich hardwood forests, with a strong onion-garlic smell and a reddish stem.

Caution. Protected in Quebec: no sale, and a personal limit of 50 plants a year. Cut one leaf per plant elsewhere.

When is ramps in season in Canada?

Ramps is usually in season May to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does ramps grow in Canada?

Ramps grows in Ontario, Quebec. Regions: Ontario, Quebec.

How do you recognise ramps?

Broad, smooth spring leaves in rich hardwood forests, with a strong onion-garlic smell and a reddish stem.

How do you harvest ramps?

Cut a single leaf per plant with scissors and leave the bulb in the ground. Only pick from large, healthy patches.

How do you eat ramps?

Grilled, pickled, in eggs or pesto.

How do you store ramps?

Pickle the bulbs, freeze leaves as pesto.

What can be confused with ramps?

Lily of the valley (Convallaria majalis), rated deadly. Ramps and wild onions smell of onion or garlic. Lily of the valley smells of nothing when crushed. Lily of the valley has nodding white bell flowers in a row. False hellebore (Veratrum viride), rated deadly. False hellebore leaves are deeply pleated along their length. It grows from a tall stalk with many leaves; ramps have 2–3 leaves from the base. No onion smell.

What is ramps good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C.

What can you cook with ramps?

  • Pickled ramps: Ramp bulbs in a spiced vinegar brine.
