Desert raisin

Solanum centrale
Also called Bush tomato, Akudjura

Desert raisin is a wild fruit you can forage from April to July in Arid centre. Small grey, spiny desert shrub with purple flowers; fruit dry on the bush into caramel-raisin nuggets.

Caution. Only Solanum centrale and a few others are edible; many bush tomatoes are toxic. Learn from Aboriginal guides.

When is desert raisin in season in Australia?

Desert raisin is usually in season April to July. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does desert raisin grow in Australia?

Desert raisin grows in Northern Territory, Queensland, South Australia, Western Australia. Regions: Arid centre.

How do you recognise desert raisin?

Small grey, spiny desert shrub with purple flowers; fruit dry on the bush into caramel-raisin nuggets.

How do you harvest desert raisin?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat desert raisin?

Ground as a spice for damper, chutney and meat.

How do you store desert raisin?

Keeps dry for months.

What can be confused with desert raisin?

Toxic Solanums (Solanum species), rated poisonous. Many bush tomatoes and nightshades are poisonous, especially unripe. Only harvest species identified by Aboriginal guides or experts.

What can you cook with desert raisin?

  • Bush tomato kangaroo: Kangaroo fillet with a bush tomato and pepperberry rub.
