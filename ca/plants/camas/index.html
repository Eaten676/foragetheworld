Camas

Camassia quamash
Also called Camas, Camassie

Camas is a root or shoot you can forage from July to October in British Columbia. Meadow lily with grass-like leaves and spikes of blue-violet star flowers.

Caution. Death camas grows alongside and kills. Mark plants in flower and dig only blue ones.

When is camas in season in Canada?

Camas is usually in season July to October. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does camas grow in Canada?

Camas grows in British Columbia. Regions: British Columbia.

How do you recognise camas?

Meadow lily with grass-like leaves and spikes of blue-violet star flowers.

How do you harvest camas?

Mark plants while in flower so you dig only blue-flowered camas later; take a few bulbs and replant the small ones.

How do you eat camas?

Slow-roasted or pit-cooked until sweet and dark.

How do you store camas?

Dry cooked bulbs.

What can be confused with camas?

Death camas (Toxicoscordion venenosum), rated deadly. Camas has blue-violet star flowers. Death camas has creamy-white flowers in a tighter cluster. The bulbs and leaves look alike out of flower, so only dig plants marked while flowering.

What is camas good for?

Calories.

What can you cook with camas?

  • Camas pit bake: Camas bulbs slow-cooked until sweet, after Coast Salish and Nlaka'pamux tradition.
