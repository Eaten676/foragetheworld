Black locust flowers

Robinia pseudoacacia
Also called Black locust

Black locust flowers is a edible flower or tea you can forage from May to June in Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Pacific Northwest and Rocky Mountains. Tree with paired thorns and hanging clusters of white, sweetly scented pea flowers.

Caution. Eat only the flowers; bark, leaves and seeds are poisonous.

How do you recognise black locust flowers?

Tree with paired thorns and hanging clusters of white, sweetly scented pea flowers.

How do you harvest black locust flowers?

Pick flowers on a dry, sunny morning once the dew has gone, when the scent is strongest. Take a few heads from each plant so it can still set fruit.

How do you eat black locust flowers?

Fritters, or raw as a sweet nibble.

How do you store black locust flowers?

Best fresh.

What can you cook with black locust flowers?

  • Black locust fritters: Flower clusters dipped in light batter, fried and dusted with sugar.
