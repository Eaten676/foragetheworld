Dandelion

Taraxacum officinale
Also called Dandelion, Lion's tooth

Dandelion is a wild green you can forage from March to October across the United States. Rosette of jagged leaves with a single yellow flower per hollow stem that oozes white sap.

When is dandelion in season in the United States?

Dandelion is usually in season March to October. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does dandelion grow in the United States?

Dandelion grows across the United States. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Southwest, Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest, California, Alaska.

How do you recognise dandelion?

Rosette of jagged leaves with a single yellow flower per hollow stem that oozes white sap.

How do you harvest dandelion?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat dandelion?

Young leaves in salad, older ones sautéed. Flowers as fritters or wine; roasted root as coffee.

How do you store dandelion?

Dry roots and leaves; flowers into syrup.

What is dandelion good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C, Iron & calcium.

What can you cook with dandelion?

  • Dandelion fritters: Flower heads in batter, fried until golden, with honey.
