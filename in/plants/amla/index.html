Amla

Phyllanthus emblica
Also called Amla, Nellikayi, Nellikai, Avla

Amla is a wild fruit you can forage from November to February in South India, West coast and Deccan, Central forests, East, Northern plains and Himalaya. Round, pale green fruit with faint ribs, close along branches among feathery leaves.

When is amla in season in India?

Amla is usually in season November to February. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does amla grow in India?

Amla grows in Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Andhra Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, Bihar, Chandigarh, Chhattisgarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Delhi, Goa, Gujarat, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Kerala, Ladakh, Lakshadweep, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Puducherry, Punjab, Sikkim, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, West Bengal. Regions: South India, West coast and Deccan, Central forests, East, Northern plains, Himalaya.

How do you recognise amla?

Round, pale green fruit with faint ribs, close along branches among feathery leaves.

How do you harvest amla?

Shake branches over a cloth, or pick by hand. Don't lop branches.

How do you eat amla?

Raw with salt, chutney, pickle, murabba.

How do you store amla?

Salt and sun-dry slices, or make pickle. Keeps up to a year.

What is amla good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with amla?

  • Amla juice: Deseed amla, blend with water, ginger and a little honey or jaggery, then strain. Drink fresh, since vitamin C fades fast.
  • Amla pickle: Steamed amla in mustard oil with fenugreek, mustard and chilli.
  • Amla murabba: Whole amla slow-cooked in sugar syrup with cardamom.
