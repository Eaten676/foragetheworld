Malabar spinach

Basella alba
Also called Poi saag, Basale, Pasalai, Mayalu

Malabar spinach is a wild green you can forage from June to November in South India, East, West coast and Deccan and Northeast. Climber with thick, glossy, heart-shaped leaves and often red stems. Half-wild on fences.

When is Malabar spinach in season in India?

Malabar spinach is usually in season June to November. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does Malabar spinach grow in India?

Malabar spinach grows in Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Andhra Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Goa, Gujarat, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Kerala, Lakshadweep, Maharashtra, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Odisha, Puducherry, Sikkim, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Tripura, West Bengal. Regions: South India, East, West coast and Deccan, Northeast.

How do you recognise Malabar spinach?

Climber with thick, glossy, heart-shaped leaves and often red stems. Half-wild on fences.

How do you harvest Malabar spinach?

Pinch off the young tips and top leaves with your fingers or scissors. Leave the lower leaves and roots so the plant regrows. Pick in the morning, away from roads and drains.

How do you eat Malabar spinach?

Basale-bassaru, saag, dosa batter, pakoras.

How do you store Malabar spinach?

Best fresh. Blanch and freeze.

What is Malabar spinach good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C, Iron & calcium.

What can you cook with Malabar spinach?

  • Basale pakoras: Whole Malabar spinach leaves dipped in batter and fried.
  • Basale bassaru: Malabar spinach and dal stock as a spicy saaru, with the greens as palya. Two dishes from one pot.
  • Wild greens dosa: Ground rice and dal batter with a handful of greens. No fermentation needed.
