Medlar

Mespilus germanica
Also called Mispel, Nèfle, Nespolo

Medlar is a wild fruit you can forage from October to December in Swiss Plateau, Jura and Ticino and southern valleys. Small, twisted tree of old hedges and woodland edges with large white flowers and russet fruit open at one end.

When is medlar in season in Switzerland?

Medlar is usually in season October to December. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does medlar grow in Switzerland?

Medlar grows in Aargau, Appenzell Ausserrhoden, Basel-Landschaft, Basel-Stadt, Bern, Fribourg, Geneva, Graubünden, Jura, Lucerne, Neuchâtel, Schaffhausen, Schwyz, Solothurn, St. Gallen, Thurgau, Ticino, Vaud, Zug, Zurich. Regions: Swiss Plateau, Jura, Ticino and southern valleys.

How do you recognise medlar?

Small, twisted tree of old hedges and woodland edges with large white flowers and russet fruit open at one end.

How do you harvest medlar?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat medlar?

Leave picked fruit to 'blet' until soft and brown, then eat with a spoon, or make medlar jelly.

How do you store medlar?

Medlar jelly or cheese.

What is medlar good for?

Vitamin C, Calories.

What can you cook with medlar?

  • Medlar compote (Mispelkompott): Bletted medlars stewed with sugar.
