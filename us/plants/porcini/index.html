Porcini

Boletus edulis
Also called Cep, Penny bun

Porcini is a wild mushroom you can forage from August to October in Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest, California, Northeast, Midwest and Alaska. Stout mushroom with a brown, bun-like cap, a sponge of pores (no gills) under it that turns from white to yellow-green, and a fat stem with a fine white net near the top. Flesh stays white when cut.

Caution. Avoid any bolete with red pores or flesh that turns blue when cut. The bitter bolete has pink pores and a dark net and ruins a dish.

When is porcini in season in the United States?

Porcini is usually in season August to October. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does porcini grow in the United States?

Porcini grows in Alaska, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Hawaii, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, Ohio, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming. Regions: Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest, California, Northeast, Midwest, Alaska.

How do you recognise porcini?

Stout mushroom with a brown, bun-like cap, a sponge of pores (no gills) under it that turns from white to yellow-green, and a fat stem with a fine white net near the top. Flesh stays white when cut.

How do you harvest porcini?

Twist out firm, young specimens; slice in half on the spot to check for maggots.

How do you eat porcini?

Sliced and fried, risotto, soups; dried for winter.

How do you store porcini?

Slice and dry.

What can be confused with porcini?

Devil's bolete (Rubroboletus satanas), rated poisonous. Devil's bolete has red pores and a red-netted stem; porcini pores are white to yellow-green. Its flesh turns blue when cut; porcini flesh stays white.

What is porcini good for?

Protein.

What can you cook with porcini?

  • Porcini pasta: Porcini sautéed with garlic, tossed with pasta and parmesan.
