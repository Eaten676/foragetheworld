Porcini

Boletus edulis
Also called Karljohanssvamp

Porcini is a wild mushroom you can forage from July to September across Sweden. Stout mushroom with a brown, bun-like cap, a sponge of pores (no gills) under it that turns from white to yellow-green, and a fat stem with a fine white net near the top. Flesh stays white when cut.

Caution. Avoid any bolete with red pores or flesh that turns blue when cut. The bitter bolete has pink pores and a dark net and ruins a dish.

When is porcini in season in Sweden?

Porcini is usually in season July to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does porcini grow in Sweden?

Porcini grows across Sweden. Regions: Götaland (south), Svealand (middle), Norrland (north).

How do you recognise porcini?

Stout mushroom with a brown, bun-like cap, a sponge of pores (no gills) under it that turns from white to yellow-green, and a fat stem with a fine white net near the top. Flesh stays white when cut.

How do you harvest porcini?

Twist out firm, young specimens; slice in half on the spot to check for maggots.

How do you eat porcini?

Sliced and fried, risotto, soups; dried for winter.

How do you store porcini?

Slice and dry.

What can be confused with porcini?

Devil's bolete (Rubroboletus satanas), rated poisonous. Devil's bolete has red pores and a red-netted stem; porcini pores are white to yellow-green. Its flesh turns blue when cut; porcini flesh stays white.

What is porcini good for?

Protein.

What can you cook with porcini?

  • Fried porcini (stekt karljohan): Thick slices of porcini fried in butter.
