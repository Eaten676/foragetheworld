Saffron milk cap

Lactarius deliciosus / L. deterrimus
Also called Saffron milk cap, Pine mushroom

Saffron milk cap is a wild mushroom you can forage from March to May in Central North Island, Canterbury, Otago and Southland, Top of the South and Wellington and Manawatū. Orange cap with darker rings under pines and spruces; bleeds bright carrot-orange milk when cut, and bruises green.

Caution. Intermediate mushroom: learn it in person first. The woolly milk cap is poisonous and has white milk and a hairy cap edge.

When is saffron milk cap in season in New Zealand?

Saffron milk cap is usually in season March to May. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does saffron milk cap grow in New Zealand?

Saffron milk cap grows in Bay of Plenty, Canterbury, Manawatū-Whanganui, Marlborough, Nelson, Otago, Southland, Taranaki, Tasman, Waikato, Wellington. Regions: Central North Island, Canterbury, Otago and Southland, Top of the South, Wellington and Manawatū.

How do you recognise saffron milk cap?

Orange cap with darker rings under pines and spruces; bleeds bright carrot-orange milk when cut, and bruises green.

How do you harvest saffron milk cap?

Twist or cut mushrooms at the base, brush off soil in the woods and carry them in a basket, never a plastic bag. Only take ones you're 100% sure of, leave old and very young ones, and follow local limits.

How do you eat saffron milk cap?

Fried, grilled or pickled; a favourite in Spain, Poland and the Nordics.

How do you store saffron milk cap?

Pickle or cook and freeze.

What can be confused with saffron milk cap?

Death cap (Amanita phalloides), rated deadly. Death caps grow under oaks in towns and parks. They have white gills that never turn pink or brown, a ring, and a bag-like cup at the base.

What is saffron milk cap good for?

Protein.
