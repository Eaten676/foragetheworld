Sochan

Rudbeckia laciniata
Also called Sochan, Cutleaf coneflower

Sochan is a wild green you can forage from March to May in Southeast, Northeast, Midwest and Rocky Mountains. Tall cutleaf coneflower of moist bottomlands; deeply cut leaves in spring and drooping yellow petals in late summer.

When is sochan in season in the United States?

Sochan is usually in season March to May. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does sochan grow in the United States?

Sochan grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming. Regions: Southeast, Northeast, Midwest, Rocky Mountains.

How do you recognise sochan?

Tall cutleaf coneflower of moist bottomlands; deeply cut leaves in spring and drooping yellow petals in late summer.

How do you harvest sochan?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat sochan?

Young leaves boiled and fried in fat, a cherished Cherokee spring green.

How do you store sochan?

Blanch and freeze.

What is sochan good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C.

What can you cook with sochan?

  • Sochan greens: Young leaves boiled, then fried in bacon fat, the Cherokee way.
