Wild onion

Allium canadense
Also called Wild onion, Ail du Canada

Wild onion is a wild green you can forage from April to July in Ontario and Prairies. Grass-like hollow or flat leaves smelling of onion, often with a cluster of bulblets on top.

Caution. If it doesn't smell of onion, don't eat it: death camas looks similar.

When is wild onion in season in Canada?

Wild onion is usually in season April to July. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does wild onion grow in Canada?

Wild onion grows in Alberta, Manitoba, Ontario, Saskatchewan. Regions: Ontario, Prairies.

How do you recognise wild onion?

Grass-like hollow or flat leaves smelling of onion, often with a cluster of bulblets on top.

How do you harvest wild onion?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat wild onion?

Chop like chives or spring onions.

How do you store wild onion?

Dry or freeze chopped leaves.

What can be confused with wild onion?

Lily of the valley (Convallaria majalis), rated deadly. Ramps and wild onions smell of onion or garlic. Lily of the valley smells of nothing when crushed. Lily of the valley has nodding white bell flowers in a row.

What is wild onion good for?

Vitamin C.
