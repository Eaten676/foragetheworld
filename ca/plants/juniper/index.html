Juniper berries

Juniperus communis
Also called Juniper berries, Genévrier commun

Juniper berries is a wild fruit you can forage from September to March across Canada. Spiky evergreen shrub of heaths and mountains; blue-black berries take two years to ripen.

Caution. Use as a spice only; avoid in pregnancy.

When is juniper berries in season in Canada?

Juniper berries is usually in season September to March. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does juniper berries grow in Canada?

Juniper berries grows across Canada. Regions: British Columbia, Prairies, Ontario, Quebec, Atlantic Canada, The North.

How do you recognise juniper berries?

Spiky evergreen shrub of heaths and mountains; blue-black berries take two years to ripen.

How do you harvest juniper berries?

Pick only the blue-black, ripe berries (two years old), leaving green ones to ripen.

How do you eat juniper berries?

A spice for game, sauerkraut and gin.

How do you store juniper berries?

Dry.

What can be confused with juniper berries?

Yew (Taxus canadensis / T. brevifolia), rated deadly. Yew needles are flat and green on both sides with no white stripes and no resin smell. Yew has red, cup-shaped berries, not cones. Fir smells of resin; juniper berries are blue and dusty.
