Jerusalem artichoke

Helianthus tuberosus
Also called Sunchoke, Jerusalem artichoke

Jerusalem artichoke is a root or shoot you can forage from October to March in Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Rocky Mountains and Pacific Northwest. Tall sunflower with small yellow flowers; knobbly tubers under the plant, sweetest after frost.

Caution. Rich in inulin; may cause gas.

When is Jerusalem artichoke in season in the United States?

Jerusalem artichoke is usually in season October to March. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does Jerusalem artichoke grow in the United States?

Jerusalem artichoke grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest.

How do you recognise Jerusalem artichoke?

Tall sunflower with small yellow flowers; knobbly tubers under the plant, sweetest after frost.

How do you harvest Jerusalem artichoke?

Only dig roots where you have the landowner's permission, since uprooting wild plants is illegal in many places. Take a few from a large patch and backfill the hole.

How do you eat Jerusalem artichoke?

Roasted, soup, chips.

How do you store Jerusalem artichoke?

Store in damp sand.
