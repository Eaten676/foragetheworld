Kawakawa

Piper excelsum
Also called Kawakawa

Kawakawa is a edible flower or tea you can forage all year round in Northland and Auckland, Central North Island, East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū, Top of the South and West Coast. Shrub of forest edges with heart-shaped, glossy leaves often riddled with caterpillar holes, which mark the best leaves; peppery.

Caution. Use the leaves; eat the ripe orange fruit only in small amounts.

When is kawakawa in season in New Zealand?

Kawakawa is usually in season all year round. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does kawakawa grow in New Zealand?

Kawakawa grows in Auckland, Bay of Plenty, Gisborne, Hawke's Bay, Manawatū-Whanganui, Marlborough, Nelson, Northland, Taranaki, Tasman, Waikato, Wellington, West Coast. Regions: Northland and Auckland, Central North Island, East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū, Top of the South, West Coast.

How do you recognise kawakawa?

Shrub of forest edges with heart-shaped, glossy leaves often riddled with caterpillar holes, which mark the best leaves; peppery.

How do you harvest kawakawa?

Pick flowers on a dry, sunny morning once the dew has gone, when the scent is strongest. Take a few heads from each plant so it can still set fruit.

How do you eat kawakawa?

Tea, or as a peppery herb for fish and meat.

How do you store kawakawa?

Dry leaves.

What can you cook with kawakawa?

  • Kawakawa tea: Holey kawakawa leaves steeped in hot water, a traditional rongoā drink.
  • Kawakawa pesto: Kawakawa leaves blended with oil and nuts.
