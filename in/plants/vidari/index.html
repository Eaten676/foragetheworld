Vidarikand

Pueraria tuberosa
Also called Vidarikand, Bilaikand

Vidarikand is a root or shoot you can forage from November to February in Central forests, Northern plains, West coast and Deccan and Himalaya. Big woody climber of dry forests with huge round tubers. Eaten by forest communities in central India.

Caution. Learn from locals which tubers to dig, and leave mature plants.

When is vidarikand in season in India?

Vidarikand is usually in season November to February. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does vidarikand grow in India?

Vidarikand grows in Arunachal Pradesh, Bihar, Chandigarh, Chhattisgarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Delhi, Goa, Gujarat, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Jharkhand, Ladakh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Punjab, Sikkim, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand. Regions: Central forests, Northern plains, West coast and Deccan, Himalaya.

How do you recognise vidarikand?

Big woody climber of dry forests with huge round tubers. Eaten by forest communities in central India.

How do you harvest vidarikand?

Dig carefully with a trowel or digging stick. Take only part of a clump, and put the crown or a piece back in the soil so it regrows.

How do you eat vidarikand?

Peeled tuber roasted, boiled or eaten raw in small pieces.

How do you store vidarikand?

Slice and sun-dry, then powder.
