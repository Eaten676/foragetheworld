Spicebush

Lindera benzoin
Also called Spicebush, Wild allspice

Spicebush is a edible flower or tea you can forage from August to October in Northeast, Southeast and Midwest. Shrub of moist eastern woods whose twigs and leaves smell of citrus and allspice; glossy red berries in autumn.

When is spicebush in season in the United States?

Spicebush is usually in season August to October. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does spicebush grow in the United States?

Spicebush grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest.

How do you recognise spicebush?

Shrub of moist eastern woods whose twigs and leaves smell of citrus and allspice; glossy red berries in autumn.

How do you harvest spicebush?

Pick flowers on a dry, sunny morning once the dew has gone, when the scent is strongest. Take a few heads from each plant so it can still set fruit.

How do you eat spicebush?

Dried berries ground as 'Appalachian allspice'; twigs and leaves for tea.

How do you store spicebush?

Dry berries.

What is spicebush good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with spicebush?

  • Spicebush tea: Twigs and leaves simmered into a citrusy, spicy tea.
