Wild phalsa

Grewia
Also called Phalsa, Dhaman

Wild phalsa is a wild fruit you can forage from May to June in Northern plains, Central forests, Thar and dry northwest, West coast and Deccan and South India. Shrubby trees with small purple-black or orange berries in early summer.

When is wild phalsa in season in India?

Wild phalsa is usually in season May to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does wild phalsa grow in India?

Wild phalsa grows in Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Chandigarh, Chhattisgarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Delhi, Goa, Gujarat, Haryana, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Kerala, Lakshadweep, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Puducherry, Punjab, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand. Regions: Northern plains, Central forests, Thar and dry northwest, West coast and Deccan, South India.

How do you recognise wild phalsa?

Shrubby trees with small purple-black or orange berries in early summer.

How do you harvest wild phalsa?

Pick fruit that comes away easily, or gather freshly fallen fruit. Don't break branches, and leave plenty for birds and animals.

How do you eat wild phalsa?

Fresh with salt, or phalsa sharbat.

How do you store wild phalsa?

Make sharbat syrup.

What is wild phalsa good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with wild phalsa?

  • Phalsa sharbat: Mash ripe berries in water, strain out the seeds, then add sugar, black salt and roasted cumin.
