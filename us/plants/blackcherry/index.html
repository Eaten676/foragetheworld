Black cherry

Prunus serotina
Also called Black cherry, Wild black cherry, Rum cherry

Black cherry is a wild fruit you can forage from July to September in Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains and Southwest. Tall tree with dark, flaky 'burnt potato chip' bark and long drooping strings of small cherries that ripen black in late summer.

Caution. Pits, leaves and twigs contain cyanide compounds: strain out the stones and never eat wilted leaves.

When is black cherry in season in the United States?

Black cherry is usually in season July to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does black cherry grow in the United States?

Black cherry grows in Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, Nevada, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Southwest.

How do you recognise black cherry?

Tall tree with dark, flaky 'burnt potato chip' bark and long drooping strings of small cherries that ripen black in late summer.

How do you harvest black cherry?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat black cherry?

Jelly, syrup, cordial and cherry bounce; the flavour is like a richer chokecherry.

How do you store black cherry?

Juice and freeze, or make jelly.

What is black cherry good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with black cherry?

  • Black cherry jelly: Black cherries cooked, strained of their stones and set into dark jelly.
