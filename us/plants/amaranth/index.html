Wild amaranth

Amaranthus retroflexus
Also called Pigweed, Redroot amaranth

Wild amaranth is a wild green you can forage from June to September across the United States. Upright weed with oval leaves, a red root and dense green bristly flower spikes.

When is wild amaranth in season in the United States?

Wild amaranth is usually in season June to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does wild amaranth grow in the United States?

Wild amaranth grows across the United States. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Southwest, Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest, California, Alaska.

How do you recognise wild amaranth?

Upright weed with oval leaves, a red root and dense green bristly flower spikes.

How do you harvest wild amaranth?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat wild amaranth?

Young leaves cooked like spinach; seeds toasted.

How do you store wild amaranth?

Blanch and freeze leaves; dry seeds.

What is wild amaranth good for?

Iron & calcium, Vitamin A, Protein.
