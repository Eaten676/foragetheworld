Sweet gale

Myrica gale
Also called Sweet gale, Bog myrtle

Sweet gale is a edible flower or tea you can forage from May to September in Northeast, Midwest, Pacific Northwest and Alaska. Low shrub of wet bogs with grey-green, resin-scented leaves.

Caution. Avoid in pregnancy; small amounts.

When is sweet gale in season in the United States?

Sweet gale is usually in season May to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does sweet gale grow in the United States?

Sweet gale grows in Alaska, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, Ohio, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Midwest, Pacific Northwest, Alaska.

How do you recognise sweet gale?

Low shrub of wet bogs with grey-green, resin-scented leaves.

How do you harvest sweet gale?

Pick flowers on a dry, sunny morning once the dew has gone, when the scent is strongest. Take a few heads from each plant so it can still set fruit.

How do you eat sweet gale?

Old beer flavouring (gale); leaves as a herb.

How do you store sweet gale?

Dry.
