Wintergreen

Gaultheria procumbens
Also called Wintergreen, Thé des bois, Gaulthérie couchée

Wintergreen is a wild fruit you can forage from September to May in Ontario, Quebec and Atlantic Canada. Low evergreen of acidic woods with glossy oval leaves that smell of wintergreen when crushed and bright red berries through winter.

Caution. Contains methyl salicylate: avoid if allergic to aspirin, and keep tea weak.

When is wintergreen in season in Canada?

Wintergreen is usually in season September to May. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does wintergreen grow in Canada?

Wintergreen grows in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, Ontario, Prince Edward Island, Quebec. Regions: Ontario, Quebec, Atlantic Canada.

How do you recognise wintergreen?

Low evergreen of acidic woods with glossy oval leaves that smell of wintergreen when crushed and bright red berries through winter.

How do you harvest wintergreen?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat wintergreen?

Berries as a minty snack; leaves fermented for tea.

How do you store wintergreen?

Dry leaves after fermenting.

What is wintergreen good for?

Vitamin C.
