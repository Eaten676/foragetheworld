Maple sap

Acer saccharum
Also called Sugar maple, Eau d'érable

Maple sap is a sap or starch you can forage from February to March in Ontario, Quebec and Atlantic Canada. Large tree with five-lobed leaves; sap runs in late winter when days thaw and nights freeze.

Caution. Only tap trees over 25 cm across, with permission.

When is maple sap in season in Canada?

Maple sap is usually in season February to March. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does maple sap grow in Canada?

Maple sap grows in New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, Ontario, Prince Edward Island, Quebec. Regions: Ontario, Quebec, Atlantic Canada.

How do you recognise maple sap?

Large tree with five-lobed leaves; sap runs in late winter when days thaw and nights freeze.

How do you harvest maple sap?

Drill a 5/16-inch hole 5 cm deep on the sunny side, tap in a spile and hang a clean bucket. Collect daily and keep the sap cold.

How do you eat maple sap?

Drink fresh sap or boil 40:1 into syrup.

How do you store maple sap?

Freeze sap; can or bottle syrup.

What is maple sap good for?

Calories.

What can you cook with maple sap?

  • Maple water: Fresh maple sap drunk cold in sugaring season.
  • Maple taffy (tire d'érable): Hot maple syrup poured on snow and rolled onto a stick.
  • Pouding chômeur with maple: Quebec cake baked in maple syrup sauce.
