Butternut

Juglans cinerea
Also called Butternut, White walnut

Butternut is a wild nut you can forage from September to October in Northeast, Midwest and Southeast. Walnut cousin with pale grey bark and sticky, oblong nuts; rich buttery kernels.

Caution. Endangered by canker in many states: only gather fallen nuts and never cut trees.

When is butternut in season in the United States?

Butternut is usually in season September to October. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does butternut grow in the United States?

Butternut grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Midwest, Southeast.

How do you recognise butternut?

Walnut cousin with pale grey bark and sticky, oblong nuts; rich buttery kernels.

How do you harvest butternut?

Gather nuts from the ground once they fall, discarding any with holes or mould. Leave plenty for wildlife, which depends on them for winter.

How do you eat butternut?

Cured and cracked like black walnuts; pickled green in early summer.

How do you store butternut?

Cure nuts.

What is butternut good for?

Fat, Protein.
