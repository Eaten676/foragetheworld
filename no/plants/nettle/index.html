Stinging nettle

Urtica dioica
Also called Brennesle

Stinging nettle is a wild green you can forage from April to July in Eastern Norway, Southern Norway, Western Norway and Trøndelag. Upright plant with toothed, heart-shaped leaves covered in stinging hairs, in rich soil near paths, ruins and streams.

When is stinging nettle in season in Norway?

Stinging nettle is usually in season April to July. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does stinging nettle grow in Norway?

Stinging nettle grows in Agder, Akershus, Buskerud, Innlandet, Møre og Romsdal, Oslo, Rogaland, Telemark, Trøndelag, Vestfold, Vestland, Østfold. Regions: Eastern Norway, Southern Norway, Western Norway, Trøndelag.

How do you recognise stinging nettle?

Upright plant with toothed, heart-shaped leaves covered in stinging hairs, in rich soil near paths, ruins and streams.

How do you harvest stinging nettle?

Wear gloves and snip the top four to six leaves of young plants before they flower. Cut back old patches in early summer for a second crop of tender tips.

How do you eat stinging nettle?

Soup, risotto, pesto, tea. Pick the top four leaves with gloves.

How do you store stinging nettle?

Blanch and freeze, or dry for tea.

What is stinging nettle good for?

Protein, Iron & calcium, Vitamin A.

What can you cook with stinging nettle?

  • Nettle soup (nesle suppe): Young nettles in a creamy soup with a boiled egg.
