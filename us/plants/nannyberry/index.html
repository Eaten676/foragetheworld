Nannyberry

Viburnum lentago
Also called Nannyberry, Sheepberry

Nannyberry is a wild fruit you can forage from September to November in Northeast, Midwest and Great Plains. Small tree of wet edges with fine-toothed leaves on winged stalks and drooping clusters of blue-black berries in autumn.

When is nannyberry in season in the United States?

Nannyberry is usually in season September to November. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does nannyberry grow in the United States?

Nannyberry grows in Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Dakota, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Midwest, Great Plains.

How do you recognise nannyberry?

Small tree of wet edges with fine-toothed leaves on winged stalks and drooping clusters of blue-black berries in autumn.

How do you harvest nannyberry?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat nannyberry?

Date-like flesh around a flat seed; eat raw or push through a sieve for fruit leather and butter.

How do you store nannyberry?

Dry as leather.

What is nannyberry good for?

Calories.
