Chestnut

Castanea spp.
Also called Chestnut, Feral sweet chestnut, Chinese chestnut

Chestnut is a wild nut you can forage from September to October in Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Pacific Northwest and California. Large tree with long, saw-toothed leaves; glossy brown nuts inside very spiny green cases.

Caution. Horse chestnuts (conkers) are poisonous: they have one round nut in a case with few, blunt spikes.

When is chestnut in season in the United States?

Chestnut is usually in season September to October. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does chestnut grow in the United States?

Chestnut grows in Alabama, Arkansas, California, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Pacific Northwest, California.

How do you recognise chestnut?

Large tree with long, saw-toothed leaves; glossy brown nuts inside very spiny green cases.

How do you harvest chestnut?

Gather fallen nuts with gloves or by rolling the spiny cases open with your boots; choose plump, shiny nuts.

How do you eat chestnut?

Roasted, chestnut purée, flour, stuffing.

How do you store chestnut?

Freeze peeled, or dry and grind.

Calories.
