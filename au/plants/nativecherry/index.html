Native cherry

Exocarpos cupressiformis
Also called Native cherry

Native cherry is a wild fruit you can forage from November to February in Subtropical east coast, Temperate south-east, South-west and Tasmania. Small, cypress-like tree of dry eucalypt forest; the tiny green nut sits on a swollen red or yellow stalk.

When is native cherry in season in Australia?

Native cherry is usually in season November to February. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does native cherry grow in Australia?

Native cherry grows across Australia. Regions: Subtropical east coast, Temperate south-east, South-west, Tasmania.

How do you recognise native cherry?

Small, cypress-like tree of dry eucalypt forest; the tiny green nut sits on a swollen red or yellow stalk.

How do you harvest native cherry?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat native cherry?

The juicy stalk is eaten fresh; discard the hard nut.

How do you store native cherry?

Best fresh.

What is native cherry good for?

Vitamin C.
