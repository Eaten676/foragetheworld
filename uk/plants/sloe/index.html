Sloe

Prunus spinosa
Also called Sloe, Blackthorn

Sloe is a wild fruit you can forage from September to November across the UK. Dense, very thorny hedge shrub with white blossom before the leaves; dusty blue-black fruit, mouth-puckering raw.

Caution. Don't crush the stones.

When is sloe in season in the UK?

Sloe is usually in season September to November. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does sloe grow in the UK?

Sloe grows across the UK. Regions: South East England, South West England, Midlands and East, Northern England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland.

How do you recognise sloe?

Dense, very thorny hedge shrub with white blossom before the leaves; dusty blue-black fruit, mouth-puckering raw.

How do you harvest sloe?

Pick after the first frost, or pick and freeze them; wear gloves against the long thorns.

How do you eat sloe?

Sloe gin, sloe jelly, sloe syrup. Best after a frost.

How do you store sloe?

Freeze to soften.

What is sloe good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with sloe?

  • Sloe gin: Pricked sloes steeped in gin with sugar from October until Christmas.
  • Hedgerow jelly: Autumn's hedgerow fruits cooked together and strained.
