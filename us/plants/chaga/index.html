Chaga

Inonotus obliquus
Also called Chaga

Chaga is a wild mushroom you can forage all year round in Northeast, Midwest and Alaska. Black, cracked, charcoal-like canker on living birch trees, rusty orange inside.

Caution. High in oxalates: drink occasionally, not if you have kidney problems or take blood thinners. Only harvest from living birch with permission, and take part of it.

When is chaga in season in the United States?

Chaga is usually in season all year round. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does chaga grow in the United States?

Chaga grows in Alaska, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Midwest, Alaska.

How do you recognise chaga?

Black, cracked, charcoal-like canker on living birch trees, rusty orange inside.

How do you harvest chaga?

Twist or cut mushrooms at the base, brush off soil in the woods and carry them in a basket, never a plastic bag. Only take ones you're 100% sure of, leave old and very young ones, and follow local limits.

How do you eat chaga?

Simmered for hours as a dark, earthy tea.

How do you store chaga?

Break into chunks and dry.

What can you cook with chaga?

  • Chaga tea: Chaga chunks simmered for a few hours into a dark, earthy brew.
