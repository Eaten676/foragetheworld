Nannyberry

Viburnum lentago
Also called Nannyberry, Viorne flexible, Alisier

Nannyberry is a wild fruit you can forage from September to November in Ontario, Quebec, Prairies and Atlantic Canada. Small tree of wet edges with fine-toothed leaves on winged stalks and drooping clusters of blue-black berries in autumn.

When is nannyberry in season in Canada?

Nannyberry is usually in season September to November. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does nannyberry grow in Canada?

Nannyberry grows in Alberta, Manitoba, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, Ontario, Prince Edward Island, Quebec, Saskatchewan. Regions: Ontario, Quebec, Prairies, Atlantic Canada.

How do you recognise nannyberry?

Small tree of wet edges with fine-toothed leaves on winged stalks and drooping clusters of blue-black berries in autumn.

How do you harvest nannyberry?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat nannyberry?

Date-like flesh around a flat seed; eat raw or push through a sieve for fruit leather and butter.

How do you store nannyberry?

Dry as leather.

What is nannyberry good for?

Calories.

What can you cook with nannyberry?

  • Nannyberry fruit leather: Sieved nannyberry pulp dried into chewy leather.
