Rock samphire

Crithmum maritimum
Also called Rock samphire, Craobhraic

Rock samphire is a wild green you can forage from May to August in Munster and Leinster. Fleshy, finely divided blue-green leaves on sea cliffs and shingle, smelling of carrot and petrol.

Caution. Grows on cliffs: never climb to reach it.

When is rock samphire in season in Ireland?

Rock samphire is usually in season May to August. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does rock samphire grow in Ireland?

Rock samphire grows in Carlow, Clare, Cork, Dublin, Kerry, Kildare, Kilkenny, Laois, Limerick, Longford, Louth, Meath, Offaly, Tipperary, Waterford, Westmeath, Wexford, Wicklow. Regions: Munster, Leinster.

How do you recognise rock samphire?

Fleshy, finely divided blue-green leaves on sea cliffs and shingle, smelling of carrot and petrol.

How do you harvest rock samphire?

Snip young shoots from plants you can reach safely; never climb cliffs.

How do you eat rock samphire?

Pickled, or blanched with fish.

How do you store rock samphire?

Pickle.

What is rock samphire good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with rock samphire?

  • Pickled rock samphire: Rock samphire in spiced vinegar.
