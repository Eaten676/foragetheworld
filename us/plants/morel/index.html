Morel

Morchella spp.
Also called Morel

Morel is a wild mushroom you can forage from April to June across the United States. Honeycomb-capped spring mushroom in old orchards, river bottoms, dying elms and burned forests. Cut it in half: a true morel is completely hollow from tip to base.

Caution. Intermediate mushroom: learn it in person with an experienced forager before eating, and always cook it. False morels (Gyromitra) are brain-like, stuffed with cottony fibres, and can kill.

When is morel in season in the United States?

Morel is usually in season April to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does morel grow in the United States?

Morel grows across the United States. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Southwest, Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest, California, Alaska.

How do you recognise morel?

Honeycomb-capped spring mushroom in old orchards, river bottoms, dying elms and burned forests. Cut it in half: a true morel is completely hollow from tip to base.

How do you harvest morel?

Twist or cut mushrooms at the base, brush off soil in the woods and carry them in a basket, never a plastic bag. Only take ones you're 100% sure of, leave old and very young ones, and follow local limits.

How do you eat morel?

Sautéed in butter, in cream sauce, or stuffed. Always cook.

How do you store morel?

Dry on a string.

What can be confused with morel?

False morel (Gyromitra esculenta), rated deadly. Slice it in half from top to bottom: a true morel is one continuous hollow chamber. False morels are wrinkled and brain-like rather than pitted, and are stuffed with cottony fibres. Morel caps attach directly to the stem; half-free morels hang like a skirt but are still hollow.

What is morel good for?

Protein, Iron & calcium.

What can you cook with morel?

  • Fried morels: Halved morels dredged in flour or cracker crumbs and fried in butter, a Midwest spring ritual.
  • Morel cream pasta: Morels sautéed in butter with shallot and cream over pasta.
