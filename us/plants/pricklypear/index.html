Prickly pear

Opuntia
Also called Prickly pear, Nopal, Tuna

Prickly pear is a wild fruit you can forage from July to October in Southwest, California, Great Plains and Southeast. Cactus with flat pads; red to purple fruit (tunas) in late summer. Young pads (nopales) are eaten too.

Caution. Burn or scrub off the tiny glochid spines with tongs before handling.

When is prickly pear in season in the United States?

Prickly pear is usually in season July to October. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does prickly pear grow in the United States?

Prickly pear grows in Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, California, Colorado, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, Montana, Nebraska, Nevada, New Mexico, North Carolina, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Virginia, West Virginia. Regions: Southwest, California, Great Plains, Southeast.

How do you recognise prickly pear?

Cactus with flat pads; red to purple fruit (tunas) in late summer. Young pads (nopales) are eaten too.

How do you harvest prickly pear?

Use tongs and thick gloves; twist off ripe red fruit and roll them in sand to remove spines.

How do you eat prickly pear?

Fruit as juice, syrup or agua fresca; pads grilled.

How do you store prickly pear?

Freeze juice.

What is prickly pear good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with prickly pear?

  • Prickly pear agua fresca: Blend peeled tunas with water and lime, strain out the seeds and serve over ice.
  • Nopales salad: Diced, de-spined pads boiled and tossed with tomato, onion, coriander and lime.
