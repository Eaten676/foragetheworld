Field mushroom

Agaricus campestris
Also called Field mushroom

Field mushroom is a wild mushroom you can forage from August to October across the UK. White cap on old, unfertilised pasture after warm late-summer rain; pink gills turning chocolate brown, and a simple ring.

Caution. Intermediate mushroom: learn it in person first. Destroying angels are pure white with white gills and a bag at the base; yellow stainers bruise yellow and smell of ink.

When is field mushroom in season in the UK?

Field mushroom is usually in season August to October. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does field mushroom grow in the UK?

Field mushroom grows across the UK. Regions: South East England, South West England, Midlands and East, Northern England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland.

How do you recognise field mushroom?

White cap on old, unfertilised pasture after warm late-summer rain; pink gills turning chocolate brown, and a simple ring.

How do you harvest field mushroom?

Twist or cut mushrooms at the base, brush off soil in the woods and carry them in a basket, never a plastic bag. Only take ones you're 100% sure of, leave old and very young ones, and follow local limits.

How do you eat field mushroom?

Fried for breakfast, on toast or in soup.

How do you store field mushroom?

Cook and freeze.

What can be confused with field mushroom?

Destroying angel and death cap (Amanita virosa / A. phalloides), rated deadly. Field mushrooms have pink to chocolate-brown gills; amanitas keep white gills. Dig up the whole base: amanitas grow from a cup-like bag (volva). Grows in woods, not open pasture.

What is field mushroom good for?

Protein.

What can you cook with field mushroom?

  • Mushrooms on toast: Field mushrooms fried in butter on sourdough.
