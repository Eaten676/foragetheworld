Laver (wild nori)

Porphyra / Pyropia spp.
Also called Laver (wild nori), Wild nori, Black seaweed

Laver (wild nori) is a wild green you can forage from March to June in Northeast, Pacific Northwest, California and Alaska. Thin, glossy purple-black sheets of seaweed clinging to rocks.

When is laver (wild nori) in season in the United States?

Laver (wild nori) is usually in season March to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does laver (wild nori) grow in the United States?

Laver (wild nori) grows in Alaska, California, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Hawaii, Idaho, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia. Regions: Northeast, Pacific Northwest, California, Alaska.

How do you recognise laver (wild nori)?

Thin, glossy purple-black sheets of seaweed clinging to rocks.

How do you harvest laver (wild nori)?

Peel sheets off rocks at low tide, taking a little from many rocks.

How do you eat laver (wild nori)?

Laverbread: cooked for hours into a dark purée, fried with oats.

How do you store laver (wild nori)?

Dry.

What is laver (wild nori) good for?

Iron & calcium, Protein, Vitamin A.

What can you cook with laver (wild nori)?

  • Wild nori furikake: Toasted, crumbled wild nori mixed with sesame and salt.
