Native celery

Apium prostratum
Also called Native celery

Native celery is a wild green you can forage all year round across New Zealand. Low coastal plant with glossy, celery-scented leaves; Cook's crew ate it against scurvy.

Caution. Hemlock is related: crush a leaf and check for a clean celery smell.

When is native celery in season in New Zealand?

Native celery is usually in season all year round. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does native celery grow in New Zealand?

Native celery grows across New Zealand. Regions: Northland and Auckland, Central North Island, East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū, Top of the South, West Coast, Canterbury, Otago and Southland.

How do you recognise native celery?

Low coastal plant with glossy, celery-scented leaves; Cook's crew ate it against scurvy.

How do you harvest native celery?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat native celery?

Herb for soups and fish.

How do you store native celery?

Dry.

What is native celery good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with native celery?

  • Green-lipped mussel chowder: Creamy chowder with green-lipped mussels and native celery.
