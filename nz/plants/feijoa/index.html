Feijoa

Acca sellowiana
Also called Feijoa

Feijoa is a wild fruit you can forage from March to June in Northland and Auckland, Central North Island, East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū and Top of the South. Grey-green hedging shrub run wild along roads and old farms; green egg-shaped fruit that drop when ripe.

Caution. Introduced and often planted: only pick from wild trees or with permission.

When is feijoa in season in New Zealand?

Feijoa is usually in season March to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does feijoa grow in New Zealand?

Feijoa grows in Auckland, Bay of Plenty, Gisborne, Hawke's Bay, Manawatū-Whanganui, Marlborough, Nelson, Northland, Taranaki, Tasman, Waikato, Wellington. Regions: Northland and Auckland, Central North Island, East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū, Top of the South.

How do you recognise feijoa?

Grey-green hedging shrub run wild along roads and old farms; green egg-shaped fruit that drop when ripe.

How do you harvest feijoa?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat feijoa?

Scoop the pulp; crumbles, chutney, smoothies and muffins.

How do you store feijoa?

Freeze pulp.

What is feijoa good for?

Vitamin C.

What can you cook with feijoa?

  • Feijoa crumble: Scooped feijoa pulp under a buttery oat crumble.
  • Feijoa chutney: Feijoas, onion, ginger and vinegar cooked into chutney.
