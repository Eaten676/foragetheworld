Garlic mustard

Alliaria petiolata
Also called Garlic mustard, Jack-by-the-hedge

Garlic mustard is a wild green you can forage from March to June in Northeast, Southeast, Midwest and Great Plains. Invasive woodland plant: kidney-shaped first-year leaves, then tall stems with triangular toothed leaves and small white four-petalled flowers. Smells of garlic when crushed.

When is garlic mustard in season in the United States?

Garlic mustard is usually in season March to June. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does garlic mustard grow in the United States?

Garlic mustard grows in Alabama, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains.

How do you recognise garlic mustard?

Invasive woodland plant: kidney-shaped first-year leaves, then tall stems with triangular toothed leaves and small white four-petalled flowers. Smells of garlic when crushed.

How do you harvest garlic mustard?

Pick young leaves in spring and the tender flowering tops; pulling whole plants is fine where it is invasive.

How do you eat garlic mustard?

Pesto, salads, sautéed greens. Picking it helps the woods, since it is invasive.

How do you store garlic mustard?

Freeze as pesto.

What is garlic mustard good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C.

What can you cook with garlic mustard?

  • Wild garlic mustard pesto: Leaves blended with nuts, cheese, olive oil and lemon.
