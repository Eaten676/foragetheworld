Elderberry

Sambucus nigra
Also called Elderberry

Elderberry is a wild fruit you can forage from February to March across New Zealand. Shrub with corky bark and flat drooping umbrellas of tiny black berries on red stalks.

Caution. Always cook. Raw berries, stems and leaves cause nausea.

When is elderberry in season in New Zealand?

Elderberry is usually in season February to March. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does elderberry grow in New Zealand?

Elderberry grows across New Zealand. Regions: Northland and Auckland, Central North Island, East Coast and Hawke's Bay, Wellington and Manawatū, Top of the South, West Coast, Canterbury, Otago and Southland.

How do you recognise elderberry?

Shrub with corky bark and flat drooping umbrellas of tiny black berries on red stalks.

How do you harvest elderberry?

Cut whole drooping clusters when every berry is dark purple; strip off stalks with a fork before cooking.

How do you eat elderberry?

Cooked: syrup, soup, jelly, chutney.

How do you store elderberry?

Freeze on the stem, then fork off.

What can be confused with elderberry?

Hemlock (Conium maculatum), rated deadly. Elder is a woody shrub; hemlock is a soft plant with hollow, spotted stems.

What is elderberry good for?

Vitamin C, Vitamin A.

What can you cook with elderberry?

  • Elderberry syrup: Late-summer elderberries cooked with honey and spice.
