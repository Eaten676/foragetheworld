Warrigal greens

Tetragonia tetragonioides
Also called Warrigal greens, New Zealand spinach

Warrigal greens is a wild green you can forage from September to April in Tropical north, Subtropical east coast, Temperate south-east, South-west and Tasmania. Sprawling succulent groundcover on beaches and estuaries with thick, triangular, crystal-speckled leaves.

Caution. High in oxalates: blanch for a minute and discard the water before eating.

When is warrigal greens in season in Australia?

Warrigal greens is usually in season September to April. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does warrigal greens grow in Australia?

Warrigal greens grows across Australia. Regions: Tropical north, Subtropical east coast, Temperate south-east, South-west, Tasmania.

How do you recognise warrigal greens?

Sprawling succulent groundcover on beaches and estuaries with thick, triangular, crystal-speckled leaves.

How do you harvest warrigal greens?

Pick young tips and leaves, then blanch before eating.

How do you eat warrigal greens?

Blanch, then use like spinach in pies, pesto and stir-fries.

How do you store warrigal greens?

Blanch and freeze.

What is warrigal greens good for?

Vitamin A, Vitamin C, Iron & calcium.

What can you cook with warrigal greens?

  • Warrigal greens pesto: Blanched warrigal greens with macadamias, oil and cheese.
  • Warrigal greens and feta pie: Blanched greens in filo with feta and egg.
