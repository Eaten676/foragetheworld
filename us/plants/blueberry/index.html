Wild blueberry

Vaccinium angustifolium
Also called Lowbush blueberry

Wild blueberry is a wild fruit you can forage from July to August in Northeast, Midwest and Alaska. Low shrub on acidic, sandy or burned ground; blue berries with a star-shaped crown.

When is wild blueberry in season in the United States?

Wild blueberry is usually in season July to August. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does wild blueberry grow in the United States?

Wild blueberry grows in Alaska, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Midwest, Alaska.

How do you recognise wild blueberry?

Low shrub on acidic, sandy or burned ground; blue berries with a star-shaped crown.

How do you harvest wild blueberry?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat wild blueberry?

Fresh, muffins, pie.

How do you store wild blueberry?

Freeze or dry.

What is wild blueberry good for?

Vitamin C.
