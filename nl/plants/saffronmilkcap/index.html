Saffron milk cap

Lactarius deliciosus / L. deterrimus
Also called Edelreizker

Saffron milk cap is a wild mushroom you can forage from August to October in East and South. Orange cap with darker rings under pines and spruces; bleeds bright carrot-orange milk when cut, and bruises green.

Caution. Intermediate mushroom: learn it in person first. The woolly milk cap is poisonous and has white milk and a hairy cap edge.

When is saffron milk cap in season in the Netherlands?

Saffron milk cap is usually in season August to October. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does saffron milk cap grow in the Netherlands?

Saffron milk cap grows in Drenthe, Flevoland, Friesland, Gelderland, Groningen, Limburg, North Brabant, Overijssel, Zeeland. Regions: East, South.

How do you recognise saffron milk cap?

Orange cap with darker rings under pines and spruces; bleeds bright carrot-orange milk when cut, and bruises green.

How do you harvest saffron milk cap?

Twist or cut mushrooms at the base, brush off soil in the woods and carry them in a basket, never a plastic bag. Only take ones you're 100% sure of, leave old and very young ones, and follow local limits.

How do you eat saffron milk cap?

Fried, grilled or pickled; a favourite in Spain, Poland and the Nordics.

How do you store saffron milk cap?

Pickle or cook and freeze.

What can be confused with saffron milk cap?

Woolly milk cap (Lactarius torminosus), rated poisonous. Saffron milk caps bleed carrot-orange milk and bruise green. The woolly milk cap has white milk and a shaggy, woolly cap edge, under birch.

What is saffron milk cap good for?

Protein.
