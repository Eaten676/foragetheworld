Silver buffaloberry

Shepherdia argentea
Also called Silver buffaloberry, Bullberry

Silver buffaloberry is a wild fruit you can forage from August to October in Great Plains, Rocky Mountains, Midwest and Southwest. Thorny, silvery-leaved shrub of prairie coulees and river banks with masses of small scarlet berries.

When is silver buffaloberry in season in the United States?

Silver buffaloberry is usually in season August to October. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does silver buffaloberry grow in the United States?

Silver buffaloberry grows in Arizona, Colorado, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, Nevada, New Mexico, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, Utah, Wisconsin, Wyoming. Regions: Great Plains, Rocky Mountains, Midwest, Southwest.

How do you recognise silver buffaloberry?

Thorny, silvery-leaved shrub of prairie coulees and river banks with masses of small scarlet berries.

How do you harvest silver buffaloberry?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat silver buffaloberry?

Jelly and syrup; sweetest after a frost. A traditional Plains food.

How do you store silver buffaloberry?

Freeze or make jelly.

What is silver buffaloberry good for?

Vitamin C, Vitamin A.

What can you cook with silver buffaloberry?

  • Buffaloberry jelly: Frost-sweetened buffaloberries made into a tangy jelly.
