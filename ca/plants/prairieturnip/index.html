Prairie turnip

Pediomelum esculentum
Also called Prairie turnip, Navet de prairie

Prairie turnip is a root or shoot you can forage from June to July in Prairies. Low, hairy plant of dry prairie with five-part leaves and blue flower spikes; a starchy root under the ground.

Caution. Dig only where plentiful; native prairie is rare.

When is prairie turnip in season in Canada?

Prairie turnip is usually in season June to July. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does prairie turnip grow in Canada?

Prairie turnip grows in Alberta, Manitoba, Saskatchewan. Regions: Prairies.

How do you recognise prairie turnip?

Low, hairy plant of dry prairie with five-part leaves and blue flower spikes; a starchy root under the ground.

How do you harvest prairie turnip?

Only dig roots where you have the landowner's permission, since uprooting wild plants is illegal in many places. Take a few from a large patch and backfill the hole.

How do you eat prairie turnip?

Peeled roots eaten raw, roasted, or dried and ground into flour; a staple of Plains nations (tîpsinna).

How do you store prairie turnip?

Braid and dry.

What is prairie turnip good for?

Calories, Protein.

What can you cook with prairie turnip?

  • Prairie turnip stew: Prairie turnips simmered with bison.
