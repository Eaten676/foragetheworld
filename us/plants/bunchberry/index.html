Bunchberry

Cornus canadensis
Also called Bunchberry, Dwarf dogwood, Crackerberry

Bunchberry is a wild fruit you can forage from July to September in Northeast, Midwest, Pacific Northwest, Rocky Mountains and Alaska. Ground-hugging dogwood carpeting conifer forests: four white 'petals' in June, then a tight cluster of red berries above a whorl of leaves.

When is bunchberry in season in the United States?

Bunchberry is usually in season July to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does bunchberry grow in the United States?

Bunchberry grows in Alaska, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, Ohio, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming. Regions: Northeast, Midwest, Pacific Northwest, Rocky Mountains, Alaska.

How do you recognise bunchberry?

Ground-hugging dogwood carpeting conifer forests: four white 'petals' in June, then a tight cluster of red berries above a whorl of leaves.

How do you harvest bunchberry?

Pick only fully ripe fruit that comes away easily, into a shallow container so it doesn't crush. Leave plenty on the plant for birds and wildlife.

How do you eat bunchberry?

Mild, apple-like berries raw or cooked into sauces with tarter fruit.

How do you store bunchberry?

Best fresh.

What is bunchberry good for?

Vitamin C.
