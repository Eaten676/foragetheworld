Pickleweed

Salicornia pacifica
Also called Pickleweed

Pickleweed is a wild green you can forage from May to August in California, Pacific Northwest, Northeast and Southeast. Jointed, succulent stems on salt marshes; turns red in autumn.

When is pickleweed in season in the United States?

Pickleweed is usually in season May to August. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does pickleweed grow in the United States?

Pickleweed grows in Alabama, Arkansas, California, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii, Idaho, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Mississippi, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia. Regions: California, Pacific Northwest, Northeast, Southeast.

How do you recognise pickleweed?

Jointed, succulent stems on salt marshes; turns red in autumn.

How do you harvest pickleweed?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat pickleweed?

Raw, blanched or pickled.

How do you store pickleweed?

Pickle.

What is pickleweed good for?

Iron & calcium.
