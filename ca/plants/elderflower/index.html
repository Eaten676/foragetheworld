Elderflower

Sambucus canadensis
Also called Elder blossom, Fleur de sureau

Elderflower is a edible flower or tea you can forage from June to August in Ontario, Quebec, Atlantic Canada and British Columbia. Flat creamy-white umbrellas of tiny flowers with a musky-sweet scent in early summer.

Caution. Water hemlock and poison hemlock also have white umbels; elder is a woody shrub.

When is elderflower in season in Canada?

Elderflower is usually in season June to August. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does elderflower grow in Canada?

Elderflower grows in British Columbia, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, Ontario, Prince Edward Island, Quebec. Regions: Ontario, Quebec, Atlantic Canada, British Columbia.

How do you recognise elderflower?

Flat creamy-white umbrellas of tiny flowers with a musky-sweet scent in early summer.

How do you harvest elderflower?

Pick flowers on a dry, sunny morning once the dew has gone, when the scent is strongest. Take a few heads from each plant so it can still set fruit.

How do you eat elderflower?

Cordial, fritters, champagne.

How do you store elderflower?

Make cordial or dry flowers.

What can be confused with elderflower?

Poison hemlock (Conium maculatum), rated deadly. Elder is a woody shrub. Poison hemlock is a soft, hollow-stemmed plant. Hemlock stems are smooth with purple blotches. Crushed hemlock smells mousy and unpleasant.

What is elderflower good for?

Vitamin C.
