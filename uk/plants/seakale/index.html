Sea kale

Crambe maritima
Also called Sea kale

Sea kale is a wild green you can forage from March to May in South East England, South West England, Wales and Midlands and East. Big, waxy, blue-grey cabbage leaves on shingle beaches, with honey-scented white flower heads in early summer.

Caution. Rare in places: take only a few side shoots, never the crown, and don't pick where it's protected.

When is sea kale in season in the UK?

Sea kale is usually in season March to May. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does sea kale grow in the UK?

Sea kale grows in East Midlands, East of England, London, South East England, South West England, Wales, West Midlands. Regions: South East England, South West England, Wales, Midlands and East.

How do you recognise sea kale?

Big, waxy, blue-grey cabbage leaves on shingle beaches, with honey-scented white flower heads in early summer.

How do you harvest sea kale?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat sea kale?

Young purple shoots and flower buds, steamed and buttered.

How do you store sea kale?

Best fresh.

What is sea kale good for?

Vitamin C, Vitamin A.

What can you cook with sea kale?

  • Sea kale with hollandaise: Steamed young shoots with hollandaise, a Victorian delicacy.
