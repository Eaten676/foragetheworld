American hazelnut

Corylus americana
Also called Hazelnut, Filbert

American hazelnut is a wild nut you can forage from August to September in Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Pacific Northwest and California. Shrub with round toothed leaves and nuts in frilly papery husks; squirrels get them first.

When is American hazelnut in season in the United States?

American hazelnut is usually in season August to September. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does American hazelnut grow in the United States?

American hazelnut grows in Alabama, Arkansas, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Pacific Northwest, California.

How do you recognise American hazelnut?

Shrub with round toothed leaves and nuts in frilly papery husks; squirrels get them first.

How do you harvest American hazelnut?

Pick nuts as husks turn brown and the nut slips out, before squirrels get them; or gather fallen nuts.

How do you eat American hazelnut?

Raw, roasted, in baking.

How do you store American hazelnut?

Dry in shell.

What is American hazelnut good for?

Fat, Protein, Calories.
