Oyster mushroom

Pleurotus ostreatus
Also called Oyster mushroom

Oyster mushroom is a wild mushroom you can forage from October to May across the United States. Fan-shaped grey to white shelves on dead hardwood, with gills running down into a short off-centre stem; smells of anise.

Caution. Intermediate mushroom: learn it in person with an experienced forager before eating, and always cook it. Angel wings, a thin white look-alike on conifer wood, has been linked to deaths.

When is oyster mushroom in season in the United States?

Oyster mushroom is usually in season October to May. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does oyster mushroom grow in the United States?

Oyster mushroom grows across the United States. Regions: Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Great Plains, Southwest, Rocky Mountains, Pacific Northwest, California, Alaska.

How do you recognise oyster mushroom?

Fan-shaped grey to white shelves on dead hardwood, with gills running down into a short off-centre stem; smells of anise.

How do you harvest oyster mushroom?

Twist or cut mushrooms at the base, brush off soil in the woods and carry them in a basket, never a plastic bag. Only take ones you're 100% sure of, leave old and very young ones, and follow local limits.

How do you eat oyster mushroom?

Tear and sear, or fry until crispy.

How do you store oyster mushroom?

Dry or cook and freeze.

What can be confused with oyster mushroom?

Angel wings (Pleurocybella porrigens), rated poisonous. Angel wings are thin, pure white and grow on conifer wood. Oyster mushrooms are thicker and fleshier, usually grey-brown to cream, on hardwoods, and smell of anise.

What is oyster mushroom good for?

Protein.

What can you cook with oyster mushroom?

  • Oyster mushroom po' boy: Crispy fried oyster mushrooms in a po' boy roll.
