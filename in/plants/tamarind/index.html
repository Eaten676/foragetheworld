Tamarind

Tamarindus indica
Also called Imli, Hunase, Puli, Chinch

Tamarind is a wild fruit you can forage from January to April in South India, Central forests, West coast and Deccan, East, Northern plains and Thar and dry northwest. Huge avenue trees with brown curved pods. Young leaves and flowers are sour and edible too.

When is tamarind in season in India?

Tamarind is usually in season January to April. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does tamarind grow in India?

Tamarind grows in Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Chandigarh, Chhattisgarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Delhi, Goa, Gujarat, Haryana, Jharkhand, Karnataka, Kerala, Lakshadweep, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Puducherry, Punjab, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, West Bengal. Regions: South India, Central forests, West coast and Deccan, East, Northern plains, Thar and dry northwest.

How do you recognise tamarind?

Huge avenue trees with brown curved pods. Young leaves and flowers are sour and edible too.

How do you harvest tamarind?

Knock down or collect pods once they're brown and brittle. They rattle when ripe.

How do you eat tamarind?

Souring for rasam, puliyogare, chutney. Roasted seeds were a famine food.

How do you store tamarind?

De-seed, salt lightly, press into blocks, sun-dry. Keeps over a year.

What is tamarind good for?

Calories.

What can you cook with tamarind?

  • Imli sharbat: Soak tamarind with jaggery, strain, then add roasted cumin, black salt and dry ginger. Dilute with cold water.
  • Imli chutney: Tamarind cooked down with jaggery, dry ginger and roasted cumin.
  • Ugadi pachadi: Neem flowers, raw mango, tamarind, jaggery, salt and chilli: six tastes in one spoonful for the new year.
  • Shevgyachya shenganchi amti: Maharashtrian drumstick amti: toor dal with goda masala, kokum or tamarind and a touch of jaggery.
  • Chinch-gulachi amti: Sweet-sour tamarind and jaggery dal with goda masala. A Pune and Konkan staple.
  • Drumstick sambar: Toor dal and tamarind sambar with drumstick pods simmered until tender.
  • Puliyogare: Rice tossed in a thick tamarind paste with sesame, peanuts and curry leaves.
  • Veppam poo rasam: Fried neem flowers in a tamarind rasam. Bitter, sour and soothing.
