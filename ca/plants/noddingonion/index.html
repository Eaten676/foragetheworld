Nodding onion

Allium cernuum
Also called Nodding onion, Ail penché

Nodding onion is a wild green you can forage from May to August in British Columbia, Prairies and Ontario. Flat, grass-like leaves and a pink flower cluster that droops like a shepherd's crook, on dry slopes and prairie. Strong onion smell.

Caution. Never pick an onion without the onion smell: death camas grows in the same places and kills.

When is nodding onion in season in Canada?

Nodding onion is usually in season May to August. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does nodding onion grow in Canada?

Nodding onion grows in Alberta, British Columbia, Manitoba, Ontario, Saskatchewan. Regions: British Columbia, Prairies, Ontario.

How do you recognise nodding onion?

Flat, grass-like leaves and a pink flower cluster that droops like a shepherd's crook, on dry slopes and prairie. Strong onion smell.

How do you harvest nodding onion?

Pinch off young tips and top leaves before the plant flowers, when they are tender and mild. Leave the roots and lower leaves so it regrows. Pick away from roads, sprayed verges and dog-walking paths.

How do you eat nodding onion?

Leaves and small bulbs raw or cooked like green onions; a traditional food in BC and the Prairies.

How do you store nodding onion?

Best fresh.

What can be confused with nodding onion?

Death camas (Toxicoscordion venenosum), rated deadly. Camas has blue-violet star flowers. Death camas has creamy-white flowers in a tighter cluster. The bulbs and leaves look alike out of flower, so only dig plants marked while flowering.

What is nodding onion good for?

Vitamin C.
