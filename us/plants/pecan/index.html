Pecan

Carya illinoinensis
Also called Pecan

Pecan is a wild nut you can forage from October to November in Southeast, Great Plains and Southwest. Large river-bottom tree in the South; nuts fall in autumn from four-part husks.

When is pecan in season in the United States?

Pecan is usually in season October to November. Timing shifts with weather, latitude and altitude.

Where does pecan grow in the United States?

Pecan grows in Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, Colorado, Florida, Georgia, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, Montana, Nebraska, Nevada, New Mexico, North Carolina, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Virginia, West Virginia. Regions: Southeast, Great Plains, Southwest.

How do you recognise pecan?

Large river-bottom tree in the South; nuts fall in autumn from four-part husks.

How do you harvest pecan?

Gather nuts from the ground once they fall, discarding any with holes or mould. Leave plenty for wildlife, which depends on them for winter.

How do you eat pecan?

Pie, pralines, toasted.

How do you store pecan?

Dry in shell; freeze shelled.

What is pecan good for?

Fat, Protein, Calories.

What can you cook with pecan?

  • Roasted pecans: Pecans toasted with butter, salt and a little cayenne.
  • Pecan pie: Pecans in a caramel custard filling.
